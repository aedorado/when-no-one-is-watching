# Chapter 10: Bypassing the Gatekeeper

When we look at the prayers of Prahlāda Mahārāja in the seventh canto of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (7.9.28), we find a foundational principle of our entire philosophy. 

Prahlāda Mahārāja is a small, five-year-old boy standing before Lord Nṛsiṁhadeva. The Lord is highly pleased with his devotion and tells him, “Ask from Me any benediction you desire”. Prahlāda has the opportunity to ask for anything in the three worlds—unlimited wealth, direct liberation, or material kingdoms.[1] 

But Prahlāda Mahārāja refuses to ask for any material blessings for himself. Instead, he kneels down, offers his respects, and prays to the Lord:

> *evaṁ janaṁ nipatitaṁ prabhavāhi-kūpe*
> *kāmābhikāmam anu yaḥ prapatan prasaṅgāt*
> *kṛtvātmasāt surarṣiṇā bhagavan gṛhītaḥ*
> *so ’haṁ kathaṁ nu visṛje tava bhṛtya-sevām*[2]

Translation by Śrīla Prabhupāda:

**“My dear Lord, O Supreme Personality of Godhead, because of my association with material desires, one after another, I was gradually falling into a blind well full of snakes, following the general populace. But Your servant Nārada Muni kindly accepted me as his disciple and instructed me how to achieve this transcendental position. Therefore, my first duty is to serve him. How could I leave his service?”**

Just look at the association contained in this one verse. The Supreme Lord Himself is here. Nārada Muni is here. Prahlāda Mahārāja is here. Śrīla Prabhupāda is here, giving us the translation. Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura is here too, echoing the same conclusion in his own song: *“tāṅdera caraṇa sevi bhakta-sane bāsa / janame janame hoy ei abhilāṣa”*—one should not be anxious to offer direct service to the Lord; the first service must be rendered to the spiritual master.[3] One single verse, and it is sufficient by itself to make anyone a pure devotee, if only we hear it attentively.[4]

This is the ultimate, unyielding standard of discipleship. Prahlāda Mahārāja is teaching us that even when the Supreme Lord Himself stands directly in front of you, offering you personal blessings and direct access, you must never try to leap over or bypass the spiritual master. 

Why is this the standard? Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, in his commentary on this verse, gives the reason: *“yasya prasādād bhagavat-prasādaḥ”*—it is only by the mercy of the spiritual master that the mercy of the Lord comes at all.[5] That is why Prahlāda himself prays, in this very verse: “How could I give up his service?”

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu Himself confirms exactly the same principle. While instructing Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, He said: *“guru-kṛṣṇa-prasāde pāya bhakti-latā-bīja”*—the seed of devotional service is obtained by the mercy of the Guru, and only then by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa. This is the secret of success.[6]

So the science is this: the spiritual master is the authorized guide, the representative of the Supreme Lord, through whose mercy alone we learn to approach and please Kṛṣṇa. We cannot bypass him to approach the Lord independently. 

Even people who are not devotees at all, but who are simply wise in the ways of the world, say this much: we should not cut off the hand that feeds us. If we cut off the hand that feeds us, who is going to feed us tomorrow?[7] The mercy of a bona fide spiritual master is exactly like that. It should never be forgotten.

I must tell you straight: **We don't know Kṛṣṇa. We don't understand Kṛṣṇa. Not that we don't understand Kṛṣṇa—we don't understand ourselves!** We don't know who we are, what is our duty, or what is our relationship with Kṛṣṇa. It is the spiritual master who gives us the training, the vision, and the qualification to approach the Supreme Lord.

## **Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura's Test of Loyalty**

That same Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura gives us an even sharper example of this principle, from his own life. He was a pure devotee—Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu Himself had already given him Kṛṣṇa-prema. When he came to Lokanātha Gosvāmī for initiation, Lokanātha Gosvāmī tested him first: *“My dear Narottama dāsa, there is no need for you to approach me for initiation. Your Guru, Caitanya Mahāprabhu, has already made you a pure devotee. You have already received Kṛṣṇa-prema. What are you looking for again from me?”*[8]

Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura answered: *“Gurudev, it is true that since Caitanya Mahāprabhu has already given me prema, I do not need initiation for that. But Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who gave me the prema, He also has a spiritual master. Therefore I am qualified to receive initiation from you.”*

Lokanātha Gosvāmī was overjoyed that his disciple had passed the test, and he initiated him. Even a devotee who had already received the highest gift directly from the Lord Himself refused to surpass the spiritual master. That is loyalty. That is submission.

## **The Guru as the Soldier of Our Ignorance**

Why is the spiritual master completely indispensable to our lives?

Because we are blind. All conditioned souls are born into this material world suffering from a thick, heavy, and sticky “cataract” covering our inner vision. Because of this spiritual cataract, we identify ourselves with these temporary bodies of skin and bones, completely unable to see our actual spiritual identity or perceive the presence of Kṛṣṇa.

Can a blind man perform surgery on his own eye?

If you have a cataract, can you take a knife, sit in front of a mirror, and operate on your own eyeball? No, of course not! If you attempt to do that, you will only blind yourself permanently. You must submit yourself to an expert who knows how to operate on the eye and remove the obstruction.

The spiritual master is like a soldier. He is authorized by the disciplic succession to fight our ignorance and operate on our blind eyes. Through his sharp, heavy instructions, his daily chiding, and his diligent watch over our practice, he removes the cataract from our eyes. It is a painful surgery. Sometimes, when the Guru corrects us, our false ego bleeds, and we feel wounded. But that purification is the only way our vision will be restored so that we can finally see Kṛṣṇa.

This is exactly what Prahlāda Mahārāja himself is saying to the Lord in his own prayer: “How can I leave his service? I am talking to You directly, I am seeing You directly—why? Because Nārada Muni gave me the eye. How can I forget the person who has given me the eye to see You?”[9]

## **Even Among Pure Devotees, Kṛṣṇa Discriminates**

Consider something extraordinary. Lord Kṛṣṇa appeared to Devakī in Mathurā, but He did not stay there—He left for Vṛndāvana to be raised by Mother Yaśodā instead. Why?

Devakī herself explained it: she had worshiped Lord Brahmā simply to get a child of her own. Yaśodāmayī also worshiped Lord Brahmā, but she prayed to bring Kṛṣṇa down to bless the entire universe. Because her love was superior, Kṛṣṇa left Devakī and stayed with Yaśodā.[10]

If Kṛṣṇa Himself discriminates like this—even among His own pure devotees—what about us, who know nothing about Kṛṣṇa at all? How can we possibly expect to approach Him directly?

This is exactly why, as we have already seen, a spiritual master is not optional; he is compulsory. Here is the same truth from a different angle. In the Upaniṣads you find the phrase *sa gurum evābhigacchet*—one must approach a guru, without fail.[11] Think of it like a certificate: a certificate without a seal is not valid, no matter how well it is written. The seal is what makes it official. In exactly the same way, one cannot understand Kṛṣṇa without a bona fide spiritual master. He is the seal.

## **The Science of Approach, Inquiry, and Service**

So if the spiritual master is the seal, how do we actually go about receiving it? Kṛṣṇa Himself gives the answer—speaking directly to Arjuna—in the *Bhagavad-gītā* (4.34): *tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā*.[12] Śrīla Prabhupāda has guided us beautifully on the three things required at the beginning of spiritual life, contained in this very verse:

1. **Submissive Approach (*praṇipāta*):** We must go to a bona fide spiritual master.
2. **Submissive Inquiry (*paripraśna*):** We must present our inquiries with a submissive heart. Absurd inquiries and blind following are both condemned. We must ask questions which are beneficial for everyone.
3. **Service (*sevā*):** Sincere, unmotivated service is compulsory.

Three things are required; two is not complete. You can make a submissive approach, and you can ask submissive questions—but without *service*, the required purification will never come. 

And that service must be completely free from motivation—100% free from motivation! Then it is a service. Sincere service of a disciple will touch the heart of the sādhu, which is very soft. When pure, selfless service penetrates and touches his heart, a blessing automatically comes. That blessing of the Guru is the secret of success for all of us.

Without service, we are simply playing games. 

Today, we are missing this science. I see some devotees trying to keep **one leg inside and one leg outside** the disciplic process. They stay in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, but they go to hear from other unauthorized persons outside of our disciplic line. 

They think: *“Oh, let me hear from here, and let me hear from there. What is the harm?”* 

But those unauthorized persons do not follow the principles, and hearing from them is a sheer waste of time. The moment you start sādhu-hopping and hearing from different places, your mind starts to discriminate. You begin to compare, and from that discrimination, offenses naturally arise. You start finding faults in the devotees and the disciplic succession. 

Eventually, both of your legs end up outside the temple altogether!

## **Servant of the Servant of the Servant**

To protect ourselves from this danger, we must strengthen our understanding of being the **“servant of the servant of the servant”**—*gopī-bhartuḥ pada-kamalayor dāsa-dāsānudāsaḥ*.[13] Serving the Guru’s followers and associates is the true meaning of guru-pūjā.

Kṛṣṇa Himself told Durvāsā Muni, in the ninth canto, that He is not pleased by direct worship that bypasses His devotees—that even the devotees of His devotee are very dear to Him.[14]

Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī explained why. When Kṛṣṇa sees that someone is sincerely serving a pure devotee, Kṛṣṇa Himself goes—not to the pure devotee, but to that servant—to take the dust of his feet and put it on His own head, to liquidate His debt to him.[15]

Think of it this way: if you love somebody, and you see that person's child in danger, and you save that child, it is not really the child you have saved—you have saved the father, and the father will be happier with you for that than if you had done the same thing directly for him. That is why the servant is also important; they are all connected, never separate.

This is why, if you sincerely serve even the servant of a sādhu, you get exactly the same results as serving the sādhu directly—because you are connected.[16]

Yet, what do we see today? Many devotees will cook one thousand and eight elaborate preparations for the spiritual master, but they will not offer a single thing to his disciples and traveling followers! 

This is the same diseased mentality we have already warned against: treating the Guru as an isolated star, separate from those around him. That is not guru-worship; that is celebrity **hero worship**! 

The spiritual master is a father who comes with a family of followers and associates. The way you treat his disciples and the junior devotees around him actually pleases him far more than how you treat him personally. True guru-pūjā means recognizing that the Guru is a package deal—the same principle Śrīla Prabhupāda captured so simply: *“Love me, love my dog.”* The followers are the sādhu’s family, and we must serve them in connection with him.

To show us how much He loves this principle, let us look at a beautiful, confidential pastime from the forest of Vraja.

One day in Vṛndāvana, Mother Yaśodā wanted to keep Kṛṣṇa home to feed Him all of His favorite preparations. She approached Him and said, *“My dear boy, tomorrow you must not go to the forest with the cows. I have made an exclusive invitation for You. You must stay home and honor this feast.”*

Kṛṣṇa looked at His mother and immediately asked, *“Does this invitation include my friends?”*

Yaśodā said, *“No, my dear. This feast is exclusively for You. I have prepared it only for You.”*

Kṛṣṇa flatly refused: **“If my friends are not invited, then I am not coming! Me means my friends! Wherever I am invited, my friends must be included, otherwise I will not accept a single bite!”**

Another time, Mother Yaśodā wanted to make a beautiful pair of shoes for Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa looked at her and said, *“If you want to make shoes for me, you must also make shoes for all my friends and all the cows.”* 

Yaśodā said, *“No, I cannot do that.”* So Kṛṣṇa refused to wear them and continued to walk bare-footed in the forest because His friends had no shoes. 

This is the sweet, absolute reality of the Lord. Kṛṣṇa never separates Himself from His friends. In the same way, the spiritual master never separates himself from his followers and disciples. If you don't have this consciousness, then you are not doing guru-pūjā at all—you are simply doing hero worship.

## **The Express Route of Sincere Devotion**

Our scriptures declare that service to a sādhu is the **express route** to liberation. 

In the material world, if you drive on a high-speed express highway, there is always a danger of terrible, fatal accidents. But on this spiritual express route, **there are no accidents and no breakdowns!** 

Why? Because you are not the driver of the vehicle! 

On this express highway, the sādhu is the expert driver. He knows the road, he knows how to navigate every turn, and he is driving under the direct guidance of the disciplic succession. For those who are under the able guidance of a bona fide sādhu, there is no fear, no accident, and no vehicle breakdown. As long as the disciple remains submissively in the vehicle, keeping their head down, chanting their rounds, and executing instructions without trying to grab the steering wheel, they are carried safely to Kṛṣṇa.

---

## **The Connection Is Spiritual, Not Material**

What happens to a disciple's relationship with the spiritual master when he is not physically present?

The connection between a disciple and the Guru should never be a material one. Materially, nothing stays forever. Spiritually, everything stays forever. Our service to the instruction of the spiritual master is more important than our service to his physical form, because the physical form does not stay forever, but his instructions live forever.

Śrīla Prabhupāda himself did not live with his own Guru. They met only occasionally. But the relationship between them was very strong, because they knew each other, and Prabhupāda knew the assignment he had been given.[17] The problem is never that we cannot physically see the spiritual master. The problem is when we never established a real relationship with him in the first place—so that the moment he is not physically present, we feel lost.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was given a specific instruction by his own Guru: to preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness in the West. He followed that instruction, and he succeeded. Those who follow the instructions given by their spiritual master are always successful—there is a guarantee of success. Without that, we simply continue to rot in the material world.[18]

## **The Disciplic Surrender of Rāmānujācārya’s Disciple**

To help us examine our own hearts, let us study a pastime from the life of the great Ācārya, Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya.

Rāmānujācārya gave his mahā-prasāda remnants to the fish.[19]

When the simple fish swallowed his holy remnants, a miracle took place. The very second those fish swallowed the remnants of the Ācārya, they instantly cast off their animal fish bodies, assumed beautiful, four-handed spiritual forms, and returned directly to the spiritual world of Vaikuṇṭha.

An intimate disciple of Rāmānujācārya, who had been traveling and serving him for many years, witnessed this and felt deeply bewildered. He knelt before his master and asked:

*“Gurudev, I have been traveling with you for years, performing constant services, fasting, and observing Ekādaśī. I have eaten your mahā-prasāda remnants a hundred times—yet my lust, my anger, and my envy are still here! I am still completely bound by my material nature. But these simple, foolish fish, who have never performed a single day of service or studied a single line of scripture, have been instantly liberated simply by swallowing your remnants! Why am I still suffering while they are delivered?”*

Rāmānujācārya looked at his disciple with great compassion and asked a single, penetrating question: 

*“My dear disciple, it is true you are traveling with me and serving me every day. But where is your consciousness? When you perform your services, where is your consciousness? Is your mind really with me, or is your consciousness on the food? Are you serving me sincerely from the heart, or are you secretly calculating: ‘When will I eat? When will I get prestige and respect? When will I become the authorized representative of my Guru?’ All these contaminations of pride, calculation, and desire for position are going on inside your heart. That is why the purification is blocked.”*

This is the ultimate mirror for our lives: we can serve for years, in every outward respect, and still find the same calculation Rāmānujācārya exposed sitting in our own hearts. And the mirror shows us something even sharper—a person serving with this kind of contaminated consciousness gets the same result whether he is serving a genuine sādhu or an ordinary person, because it is the consciousness itself, not merely who is being served, that determines the result. We must strip away all calculation and serve with a sincere, open heart.

## **Chapter 10: Heart Check**

### **What Am I Asking Kṛṣṇa For?**
Honestly reflect on the inner motivation behind your spiritual practice:

* Are you performing your services strictly to bring a smile to your Guru’s face, or are you quietly calculating how your service will get you more respect, prestige, and influence in the community?
* Do you behave like the disciple of Rāmānujācārya, performing many external rituals while your heart is meditating on when you will become the master?

### **Caring for Those Connected with the Guru**
To defeat the disease of celebrity hero-worship in our hearts:

* How do you treat the junior devotees, the traveling companions of the Guru, and those who have no social prestige? 
* *Realize:* Serving the Guru means caring for his followers and associates. If you neglect his followers while trying to worship his feet, you are not doing guru-pūjā—you are only doing hero worship.

### **Am I Hearing from the Right Sources?**

* Are you trying to keep “one leg inside, one leg outside” by running after other speakers outside our disciplic line, risking offense and discrimination?
* **The Commitment:** Next time your mind wanders or seeks outside excitement, sit submissively and read Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books, knowing that his words are sent to destroy your cataracts.
