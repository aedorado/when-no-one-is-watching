# Chapter 17: The Fire of a Spiritual Warrior

If we want to understand what it means to serve the spiritual master in separation, we do not have to look very far. We can look at the life of my beloved spiritual master, His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami. He was a spiritual warrior who had unflinching faith in his spiritual master, Śrīla Prabhupāda. His faith in his Guru increased every single moment, under any difficulties and challenges [1].

When he entered the movement, he was known as Ghanashyam Prabhu, and he threw himself completely into book distribution. Śrīla Prabhupāda loved his book distribution results so much. He mentioned many times in lectures and room conversations that book distribution gave him life. Shortly before Śrīla Prabhupāda left this planet, when he was very sick, he noted that when he heard Ghanashyam's sankirtan results, his desire to continue living increased. 

One day, when Śrīla Prabhupāda was informed that Ghanashyam had arrived, he was so eager to see him. He said, “Bring him here.” When Ghanashyam entered the room and came to his bedside, Prabhupāda was so happy that he embraced him, rubbed his head, and tears of deep affection poured from his eyes. That disciple’s life was a complete success because he brought tangible satisfaction to his spiritual master. And the satisfaction of the spiritual master is the only success for a disciple.

---

## **Maharaj’s Absorption in the Holy Name**

I met my spiritual master in the middle of a heavy kīrtana. He was very bright, very energetic, and full of spiritual power. When he led kīrtana, he danced with such physical intensity that his entire body would be drenched in sweat. After his kīrtana, we had to mop the entire temple floor because the floor was wet from his dancing [2]. Even after he finished and sat down to deliver his lecture, the mats around his seat were soaked with his sweat. 

On the day my spiritual master took sannyāsa, he was so overwhelmed with ecstatic love that during the kīrtana, he leaped high into the air. In his intense absorption, his sannyāsa dhotī slipped completely off, leaving him dancing only in his kaupīna! From that day on, to avoid any distraction while dancing for Kṛṣṇa, he started tying his sannyāsa dhotī extremely tight. Years later in Benin City, Nigeria, the kīrtana was so intense that his dhotī was completely soaked in sweat. When we went to his room, we found the knot so tightly jammed and water-logged that we struggled with it for thirty minutes—I tried, then he tried himself—until we finally had to take scissors and tear the wet sannyāsa dhotī straight off his waist!

I remember asking myself why he had tied it that tight, until I later heard His Holiness Rādhānātha Mahārāja explain it: Gurudev tied it so tight precisely because he knew he was going to dance. It reminded me of what happened to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu Himself on the day of His own sannyāsa. After the ceremony, when the kīrtana began, Mahāprabhu merged completely into the chanting—His daṇḍa flew into the air, His water pot flew in one direction, His dhotī in another, and He rolled on the floor. His own guru, Keśava Bhāratī, experienced the very same thing that day. Even His Holiness Bhaktijāgoṣa Mahārāja had this same experience on the day of his own sannyāsa. From that day on, Gurudev tied his dhotī extremely tight every single time he danced for Kṛṣṇa, and he never had that experience again. He smiled and told me: when a devotee is dancing for the pleasure of Kṛṣṇa, they must never let any material distraction interrupt their kīrtana.

He was not just speaking dry philosophy. He was addressing the deepest corners of every devotee's heart. You could not hide anything from him. He was incredibly gentle, but if he saw that you were behaving with duplicity or playing political games, he would look you directly in the eye and correct you. He would tell you directly: “You are a cheater.” He would not wait for someone to report you; he would tell you the truth directly to your face, revealing things about your own character that you yourself did not realize. This was his fatherly protection—correcting us before we fell down.

## **Preaching Under Communist Persecution**

Maharaj took incredible, life-threatening risks to distribute books in dangerous, communist countries [3]. 

During the Soviet era, when distributing religious books was a severe offense, he was once distributing books in a college in Russia. He introduced a book to a young man, who took the book and said, “Oh, very nice. This is very nice. I know someone who will buy more of these books than myself. Let me take you there.”

Maharaj went with him happily. But that man was actually a secret police officer who had been hunting down anyone distributing religious materials. Before Maharaj realized what was happening, he was surrounded by the police. They confiscated his books and threatened him with prison.

Any other person would have left the country immediately out of fear. But Maharaj only saw his Guru's order. He simply walked out of the police station, went to a different school in another part of the city, and continued distributing his books.

---

## **Maintaining Vows Under Imprisonment**

On another occasion, Maharaj was arrested in Yugoslavia. At that time, before the country was dismantled into Bosnia, Croatia, Czech, and Slovenia, the situation was extremely strict and dangerous. But Maharaj was hiding to distribute books, fully convinced that he must execute his spiritual master's orders in any condition. He did not see the danger; he only saw his Guru's order.

Locked in that cell, he faced a test far heavier than the police officers in Russia had ever posed—a test I have already told in full earlier in this book, when we looked at how to pray on the battlefield. It is enough to say here that he passed it exactly as he had passed everything else: by remembering that his Guru was watching, and refusing to play a double game.

---

## **The Mystic Power of the Guru**

My spiritual master possessed extraordinary spiritual sensitivity; he could read people like an open book. Even his knowledge of who I was seemed to arrive from somewhere beyond ordinary means. In 1982, on the day of my initiation, all the names of the new disciples were typed on a list. At that time, we had barely met—only two or three times in person—yet somehow my name was there on that list, right at the end but present nonetheless. I could not understand how he had known to include me.

That same sensitivity showed itself again, years later, in a far more personal way—the day he reached into a hidden letter without being told where it was, and taught me what it actually means to be a Maharaj. I will tell that story fully in the next part of this book, when we look at him as a caring father rather than only as a spiritual warrior.

## **The Integrity of Living What We Preach**

Maharaj wrote several books on spiritual leadership, putting his entire heart and realization into every page. He did not write books to make money; he was not a businessman.

One day, a prominent American author who had written many corporate books on leadership came across Maharaj's books [4]. He was so deeply moved that he traveled to the ashram just to watch Maharaj in person. He stayed there for a few days, quietly watching Maharaj's daily activities, his kīrtanas, and his dealings with the devotees.

Before he left, this author made a sincere confession: “I have written many books on leadership. But people who write books on corporate leadership are different from what they write, because they write books for business and money. But I have watched you for days, and I see that you are not different from what you preach. You have put your raw heart into your books.” That was the power of Maharaj’s purity—his public life and private life were completely identical.

---

## **The Standard of Devotee Care**

My spiritual master could not tolerate seeing any devotee suffer. He established a strict standard of loving care, and he expected the leaders to maintain it. 

When I first joined the movement in 1980, the temple president didn't want to recommend me for initiation. The reason was very trivial. One day, we had gone out for harināma, and the temple president was leading the kīrtana and playing the mṛdaṅga, but the devotees were not motivated. Seeing that the energy was down, one of the devotees asked the president to hand the mṛdaṅga to me. The president was highly offended by this, and out of anger and pride, he refused to recommend me for initiation!

When Gurudev arrived and observed how this president was treating the devotees, he was deeply disturbed. He saw that the president was harsh, distant, and completely negligent in caring for the devotees' hearts. Gurudev did not tolerate this corporate, business mentality. He immediately removed the president from his service, standing before him and chiding him directly: **“You cannot care for the devotees? Then what are you leading? Go back to being a normal devotee!”** 

He taught us that leadership is not about managing bricks, prestige, or files; leadership means taking active, loving responsibility for the children of Kṛṣṇa [5]. Gurudev personally stepped in, protected me, and initiated me. He showed us that a leader's primary business is to protect the devotees, and if a leader fails to do that, they have no business leading at all.

## **The Physical Hardships of the West African Mission**

In the early years, we received nothing from India. Maharaj wanted us to provide everything ourselves—making our own clay mṛdaṅgas, incense, and oils across Nigeria, Ghana, Ivory Coast, Liberia, Zimbabwe, and Zambia.

The physical hardships we went through were severe. There were very few of us, so one or two persons had to play the heavy clay mṛdaṅga for five to six hours a day, traveling from village to village under the hot African sun [6].

Because the mṛdaṅga was so heavy, I developed a very solid, painful hernia. I tried to hide the swelling and the pain because I did not want Maharaj to leave me behind or stop me from traveling with him. But when another godbrother noticed my condition and wrote to Maharaj about it, Maharaj immediately sent a strict, unhesitating command: **“Go now to the hospital and get everything checked. I am not advising you, this is an order.”** He protected his disciples' physical bodies just as fiercely as he protected their spiritual souls.

---

## **Daily Accountability and Final Selflessness**

My spiritual master modeled a life of strict, daily accountability. Every single night before he went to sleep, he would look at his diary, examine his planned activities, and check if he did everything that Prabhupāda wanted him to do. He would ask himself: *“Did I do anything, or is there anything which he wanted me to do that I did not do?”* He communicated with his Guru every day.

This uncompromised selflessness remained with him until his very last breath [7].

When Maharaj was preparing to leave his physical body, his disciples and well-wishers had raised a significant amount of money for his medical treatments and cancer care. But when Maharaj decided to stop spending money on his own failing health, he did not give the remaining medical funds to the temple for general maintenance or building projects.

Instead, he carefully divided the remaining medical funds and distributed them strictly to individual disciples and devotees who were suffering from their own physical health issues. He spent his entire life caring for the Vaiṣṇavas, and on his deathbed, he literally used his own life-saving funds to protect their bodies.

If we can follow in the footsteps of such an uncompromised spiritual warrior, and serve our spiritual masters with that exact same unhesitating, risk-taking loyalty, our lives will be an absolute success.

## **The Best Bridge Preacher**

For some days, we had been hearing the pastimes of Nārada Muni and Mahārāja Prācīnabarhiṣat. Nārada Muni came to that king to preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness, but he saw it would be too difficult for such a materialistic king to digest directly that he is not this body, he is spirit soul. So Nārada Muni preached to him through a story instead—the story of Purañjana. Nārada Muni is the best bridge preacher. He knows the consciousness of his disciples, he deals with them exactly where they are, and from there he elevates them. That is his special quality, among his other qualities.

His Holiness Bhakti Siddhānta Sarasvatī Mahārāja had that same mood, that same vitality. And if you look at it carefully, my Gurudev had it too. His teaching was always based on the consciousness of his audience. He connected with people out of compassion—the same compassion Nārada Muni used, along with mystic power. Gurudev also had mystic power, and his heart was full of compassion for the living entities. Many times, while he was giving class, he would cry, sitting right there on his own āsana. Once a devotee asked him, "What is the problem, Gurudev?" And he said: many of you are thinking of giving up Kṛṣṇa consciousness, which means you are being very wicked—you don't want to help yourself, you don't want to help your family members, and you don't want to help the world. Not being a devotee is wickedness. He was crying because of this.

That is why he established the Institute for Applied Spiritual Technology, with his seminars and workshops. Ordinarily, when you say "workshop," you mean a place where you repair something—a motorcycle, a bike, a car. But he took that same word and used it to teach people philosophy. He would train people from any level, and gradually he would raise them to the standard of becoming a devotee. Before that, you would never know he was a Hare Kṛṣṇa person—not until he had given you something and you had appreciated it. Only after some time would people ask him, "Are you a Hare Kṛṣṇa man?" And when he said yes, they would ask, "What do I do now?"—because what he had already given them, they could not find anywhere else. That is perfect bridge preaching.

An experienced salesman, if he wants to sell his goods, does not simply go out and start distributing them. First he goes to the market and looks at what interests the people. Then he designs his product accordingly, and it sells. My Gurudev knew how to raise people's consciousness toward Kṛṣṇa consciousness in exactly that way. If you read even one of his books carefully, you will see that he is preparing your consciousness for Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Most of us come into Kṛṣṇa consciousness, but not yet for Kṛṣṇa consciousness, because we do not have the foundation. His books and his practical technologies gave us that foundation first.

He would tell us: before you do anything, ask yourself—if all of us did this, how would this community be? For example, if none of us went for Maṅgala-ārati tomorrow morning, what would happen? Nobody would be there. So before thinking, "I am too tired, I don't have to go for Maṅgala-ārati today," ask yourself: if everyone thought this way, who would be there? Nobody. Therefore, drag your body to Maṅgala-ārati. That is the sacrifice we have to pay to become Kṛṣṇa conscious.

He was a personality who came to help the world with special qualities, and he was very much ahead of all of us. Fifty years ago, he was already speaking about the protection of women, the protection of children, producing our own food, and even making our own things to wear, long before we understood why any of that mattered. Now ISKCON is talking about child protection only because it has already become a crisis, and it is not working, because we were not prepared. If we had started when he was already talking about it, we would have had everything ready long before now.

A few years ago in Māyāpur, Kāntya Prabhu said, "I don't like what Bhakti Tīrtha Mahārāja did," speaking of exactly this—his talk of child protection and self-sufficiency. He didn't listen to him at the time. But now that we are finally ready, he has left us; he left us at the very time we needed him. That is what always happens. Kṛṣṇa sends His representatives here, and if we do not take advantage of them while they are with us, what will Kṛṣṇa do? He will simply take them back. Look at how many of our sincere devotees have left in exactly this way—look at Gaura Govinda Mahārāja: how did he leave? He simply walked out, disgusted. His Holiness Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja knew he was leaving; he prepared himself and left. How long are we going to keep taking advantage of what Kṛṣṇa has given us without using it properly?

## **Chapter 17: Heart Check**

### **Sincerity in the Empty Classroom**
Reflect on your spiritual standards and daily practices:

* Do I act differently when senior devotees are not physically watching me?
* Am I keeping my morning sādhana vows—rising for Brahma-muhūrta and chanting attentiveness—with the same intensity as if my Guru were standing directly in front of me?
* *The Guard Vow:* Settle this standard in your mind: *“My spiritual master has seen me. He knows whatever I am doing. I am never alone.”*

### **Willingness to Accept Correction**

* How do I react when a spiritual authority or senior devotee corrects my behaviour, my speech, or my service? Do I get defensive and angry, or do I submissively accept it as a purifying, loving intervention to save me from falling down?
* Identify one service, project, or talent that you are holding onto tightly because it brings you prestige, attention, or material comfort. Are you ready to voluntarily lay it down if your Guru asks you to?

### **Private Life vs. Public Preaching**

* Is my public behaviour on the vyāsāsana consistent with my private life when the doors are closed and no one is watching? Am I carrying a plastic, cosmetic smile, or is my heart genuinely aligned with the philosophy I preach?
* *The Purification Action:* Choose one practical, difficult, or anonymous service this week where you have zero expectation of public praise, and perform it submissively for the silent pleasure of your spiritual master.
