# Chapter 21: Stop Cooking, Start Hearing

When we gather for *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, it is not an ordinary, dry academic seminar. *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the direct sound incarnation of Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself, and hearing it is the absolute, most important activity of our eternal souls. Śrīla Prabhupāda made it this simple: "Just come and sit down and hear. That's all." The entire Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement was established to give us exactly this chance—to hear about Kṛṣṇa [13].

But how many of us are actually receptive to this sound? 

To truly hear does not mean that the sound merely hits your eardrum and falls away. It means the message riding on that sound has penetrated your ears and reached your heart—that is hearing. Sincere, attentive hearing (*śravaṇam*) is a dynamic process that purifies the heart. The moment you give genuine aural reception to *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, two things happen without fail. Every inauspicious thing within you is destroyed—the material contamination of lamentation, illusion, and fear—purifying us even of our deepest sinful habits: illicit sex, meat-eating, intoxication, and gambling, which have all become so prominent today. And at the very same time, pure love for Kṛṣṇa sprouts and grows in your heart [1]. 

But today, we have turned this into a dry, mechanical ritual. We have become expert at external, "cosmetic" behaviors, but we are empty of genuine realization and faith.

Why doesn't the sound actually go in? Because the noise of sense gratification is already too loud in our consciousness. For time immemorial, we have been accustomed to sense gratification, and it keeps ringing in our head—so when Kṛṣṇa's message comes, it cannot get through, just as a small voice cannot be heard once a horn speaker is blaring nearby. That old ringing has made our primary business—our eternal life—into something secondary, and our secondary business—this temporary body and its wants—into something primary [12].

Understand this, then: if you truly have something more important than *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* waiting for you, go and attend to it—do not pretend to sit here while your mind is elsewhere. But if Bhāgavatam really is the superior thing, then it deserves your whole attention. Put the phone away. A hearing that can be interrupted by a phone was never a real hearing to begin with.

---

## **The Glories of Gṛhastha-āśrama**

In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, we find a magnificent dialogue between the supreme emperor, Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj, and the great celestial sage, Nārada Muni. 

Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj was an eternally liberated, pure devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Every single action he performed was fully transcendental. Yet, as a householder (*gṛhastha*), he submissively approached Nārada Muni and asked a question that is deeply relevant to each one of us:

Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj asked Nārada Muni: *“The brahmacharis, sannyāsīs, and forest yogīs are free from material entanglements and constantly serve Lord Kṛṣṇa. What about us who stay in the house, surrounded by family and material responsibilities? How can we also make advancement and get free of this material entanglement?”* [2]

Listen carefully to the answer Nārada Muni gave. He looked at Yudhiṣṭhira and pointed out a beautiful, shocking paradox: 

The great forest sages perform heavy, sustained austerities (*tapo divyam*) just to catch a glimpse of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet [3]. 

But do you know what Kṛṣṇa did? He left all those forest sages behind and came to live in a house with Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj! He moved into Yudhiṣṭhira’s palace and stayed there exactly as He lives eternally in Goloka Vṛndāvana or Dwaraka—as an intimate, loving family member! 

Why? Because Yudhiṣṭhira’s pure love and unalloyed devotion were vastly superior to the dry, mechanical austerities of the forest-dwellers. 

Household life is not a spiritual death sentence; it is a glorious, powerful opportunity to conquer the Supreme Lord Himself. You do not have to run away to the forest to find Kṛṣṇa. You can turn your own home into a temple, provided you follow the formula of *sādhana* and constantly associate with saintly persons.

What actually makes the difference between the forest sage and the sincere householder devotee? A devotee depends on Kṛṣṇa. The sage depends on his own strength—and that is exactly why the sages fall down.

Look at Haridāsa Ṭhākura. As Gurudeva has told it, Māyā Devī herself came to test him, staying near him for three days, and she could not move him at all. In her defeat, she told him plainly: he had cheated her, because Kṛṣṇa was in his heart. For the great sages, she said, the moment she merely closes her eyes, they all fall and offer obeisances to her. But for him, she had stayed three days, and nothing—so she asked him, please, to initiate her instead [11].

That is the difference. The sage tries to conquer Māyā using his own austerity and strength, and Māyā—who is herself a pure devotee of the Lord, not a conditioned soul like us—effortlessly defeats him. But the devotee who simply depends on Kṛṣṇa cannot be touched, because there is no space left in his heart for anything but Kṛṣṇa Himself.

---

## **Prabhupāda’s Father and the Sādhu Hunt**

To teach us how a householder should practically build this auspicious atmosphere, we must look at the example of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s father, Gour Mohan De. 

He was a pure, dedicated householder. Every day, without fail, his daily routine included a sacred mission: he would walk out onto the busy streets of Calcutta, look for saintly sādhus and sages, and submissively beg them to come to his home for prasāda [4]. 

And when the sādhus sat in his home, what did he pray for? 

Did he approach them with a transactional list for material adjustments? Did he beg: *“Please, bless my son that he gets a prestigious corporate job, a massive bank balance, and a disease-free body”*? No! Gour Mohan De had only one, single prayer that he made every day to every sādhu:

> **“Please, bless my son that he may become a pure servant of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.”** [4]

Because of that householder’s pure, unselfish prayer, his son went on to shake the entire world and save millions of souls. That is the real duty of a *gṛhastha*. Your primary business is to use your home to associate with saintly people, hear the nectar of Bhāgavatam from them, and pray only for pure devotion.

---

## **Stop Cooking, Start Hearing!**

But let me tell you what actually happens in our modern houses. We have introduced a major, laughable disease into our society:

A sādhu arrives at a householder’s home. The family is overjoyed. But the moment the sādhu walks in and sits on the rug to speak, what do the householders do? 

The husband and the wife immediately run into the kitchen and close the door! They spend the whole of the sādhu’s visit standing over hot stoves, chopping vegetables, and frying subjis! 

Meanwhile, the sādhu is sitting alone in the living room. He is speaking *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, but there is nobody there to listen! The message of the sādhu is meant to feed our ears, but our ears are locked inside the kitchen! 

Why do we do this? Because we have a very foolish, ignorant mentality: we think the sādhu is a poor, hungry beggar, and only today he has an appetite! We think: *“Oh, this poor man has nothing to eat outside. Let me feed him nicely today. Other days he is not hungry, but today he is hungry because he came to my house!”* [5]

What nonsense! This is not real service; it is simply mistaking what the sādhu has actually come to give us. 

Kṛṣṇa is not a hungry man, and His pure representatives are not hungry beggars. They do not need your chapatis and subji. They are hungry only for your *śravaṇam*—for your submissive, attentive hearing. 

The sādhu has come strictly to destroy the ignorance in your heart by speaking about Kṛṣṇa. When you ignore his message to stand in the kitchen cooking instead, you are completely defeating the purpose of his visit. 

The Vedic standard is very clear: **Stop the cooking! Sit down on the floor, open your ears, and hear the sādhu speak first.** After you have heard attentively, and after the kathā is finished, then you can easily go and cook whatever simple thing you and the sādhu are going to eat [6]. 

And these days, if you must feed a sādhu, invite one with a big stomach! A big stomach means he is not really a sādhu—a genuine sādhu's stomach should not be outside, it should be inside. Otherwise, he is only a "chapati sādhu" [6]. Serve the genuine sādhu by opening your ears, not by filling his plate.

---

## **The Contamination of Consciousness in Food Preparation**

But there's another problem most of us never even think about: where our food actually comes from.

So many of us, especially those working outside the home, have gotten into the habit of buying food from restaurants rather than cooking at home. But that food is cooked for money, not for you—the cook's mind is on "money, money, money," not on Kṛṣṇa, and that consciousness enters the food you eat [7]. On top of that, you have no idea whether the person preparing it has even taken bath, or observed basic cleanliness, before touching your plate. You are unknowingly carrying their contamination into your own body.

This is why, as far as possible, a householder should settle the standard of cooking simple, pure meals at home—with clean hands and a heart that is crying out in love for Kṛṣṇa—protecting your consciousness from the transactional anxiety of the commercial world.

---

## **Establishing Spiritual Boundaries in Daily Life**

The human form of life is meant strictly for self-realization and returning back home, back to Godhead. Yet, under the heavy influence of the material world, look at what we are doing:

Householders are rising early in the morning, traveling long distances to their offices and factories, and working tirelessly like beasts of burden just to maintain their material standards—then spending another three or four hours in traffic just to get back home, only to retire at ten o'clock and rise early again the next day. Others, chasing a good job, are ready to make any sacrifice, working till eleven o'clock at night. When you work until eleven o'clock, what do you have left in life? When are you going to sleep? This is nothing but slavery, but people are doing it, and nobody complains! [8]

Yet ask these same people to complete their sixteen rounds of chanting, and suddenly there are a hundred excuses. We never run out of excuses for sense gratification, only for sādhana. And when they finally finish their grueling shifts, they often spend their precious free time on frivolous entertainment, phones, or worldly gossip instead of finding time to sit down and hear about Kṛṣṇa! [8]

This is a great loss. Lord Rishabhadeva warns us that this human body is not meant for working hard day and night for temporary sense gratification, which is already available to the animals [9].

We must find some time for hearing—we must find it. After all, Kṛṣṇa gave us these twenty-four hours, and He can take them away at any moment, and nobody can ask Him why. The Vedic standard is that one should work eight hours at the most to earn one's livelihood, and either in the afternoon or in the evening, a householder should associate with devotees to hear about Kṛṣṇa's activities [8].

This is Bhāgavatam. This is Kṛṣṇa consciousness. This is life. This is human life. Anything beyond this is animal life—polished animal life.

There must be a balance, and there must be a sacrifice. Your material responsibilities must not consume your spiritual life. The home and your daily schedule must leave genuine space for hearing, chanting, and family *sādhana*.

---

## **Awakening from Material Illusions**

We must wake up to the reality of our situation. This material world—our society, our family, everything we're attached to—is like a temporary, fleeting dream.

When you sleep at night, you enter a dream. In that dream, you may form a whole society of friendship and love, and it feels completely real while it lasts. But the very second your eyes open in the morning, what happens to that society? It ceases to exist! It vanishes! [10]

Similarly, this present life we are living—our gross society, our family, our love—is simply a dream in a slightly grosser form. The very second your body dies, this entire dream will be over forever [10].

Therefore, before this dream is over, let us use our homes to connect with the eternal reality.

Let us hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, again and again (*abhīkṣṇaśaḥ*). Stop playing cheap games with your spiritual life. Sit down on the floor with your family, stop the kitchen cooking, and drink the sweet, purifying nectar of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. That is the only way your home will become a brilliant, shining gateway back to Goloka Vṛndāvana.
