# Chapter 22: Giving Beyond the Modes

If you want to know how to give charity, you must study the supreme instructions of the *Bhagavad-gītā* [1]. There, Kṛṣṇa explains that everything in this material world is tinged by the three modes of nature, and our giving is no exception [2]. 

But in our daily lives, we often treat charity as a cheap social duty or a tax-saving transaction. We do not understand the subtle spiritual science behind giving, nor do we realize that whatever we possess in this material world is actually stolen property. We are all thieves who have stolen everything from Kṛṣṇa! By giving charity, we are not doing Kṛṣṇa any favor, nor are we doing the Guru any favor. It is we who get purified. Therefore, giving charity is compulsory for everyone to purify ourselves of this thief mentality.

---

## **The Three Modes of Charity**

Let us try to understand these three modes of charity carefully as Kṛṣṇa outlines them:

*   **Charity in Ignorance:** This is charity given to an unqualified person, in an impure place, and at an improper time, without respect and with neglect [3]. In the Vedic literature, there is no recommendation for giving charity indiscriminately. You cannot just throw your money anywhere and call it charity. The Vedas state that the suitable time to give charity is during a solar or lunar eclipse, at a holy place of pilgrimage, or to a qualified sādhu. Giving charity in the night is generally condemned for ordinary people because the results are inauspicious. 
    
    Giving money to support sinful activities like intoxication and gambling, or giving in an impure place like a bar, a cemetery, or a hotel, is considered to be in the mode of darkness and brings only suffering. Similarly, if a person gives charity to a suitable person but without respect and with neglect, that is also charity in ignorance.
*   **Charity in Passion:** This is charity performed with the expectation of some return, with a desire for fruitive results, or in a grudging, repentant mood afterward [4]: *“Why did I have to spend so much money on this? This was too much!”* Sometimes it is performed strictly for elevation to the heavenly planets, or simply out of obligation because a superior asked you to do it. Many charitable foundations fall into this same category—they offer their gifts to institutions with great fanfare, seeking recognition and self-glorification rather than the spiritual welfare of the recipient. I have seen this myself: people distributing grains enthusiastically on a "courtesy bed," complete with tea and coffee for the donors. What is the real idea behind it? *"I am using you to go to heaven."* That is not goodness at all—it is only passion, because the expectation of a heavenly reward is still there.
*   **Charity in Goodness:** This is charity given as a duty, without any expectation of return, at the proper time and place, and to a worthy, qualified person [5]. Goodness requires discrimination. It must be given to a qualified sādhu or a pure Vaiṣṇava devotee engaged strictly in spiritual activities.

But beyond the mode of goodness, there is **transcendental charity.** This is charity given directly to a pure devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa to facilitate and expand his preaching mission. When you beg a pure Vaiṣṇava with a humble mind to accept your offering, the result is completely transcendental. We must give with a begging heart: *“Please, kindly accept this simple offering for my own benefit.”* Because if the pure devotee refuses to accept your charity, you can never get purified. We must consider it our greatest fortune if a sādhu allows us to serve him.

---

## **The Madman’s Cigarette**

Kṛṣṇa is so merciful that He looks at our internal intention rather than our external, clumsy actions. Let me share a practical experience that happened to me personally on the streets.

One day, I was walking down a busy street, and a madman was there. He was smiling and smoking a cigarette. When he saw me, he said: *“Oh my friend, can you take some cigarettes also?”* He was sincerely offering me his cigarettes!

Now, let me ask you: will this madman benefit from his action or not? 

One devotee reminded me of a similar pastime: a madman once approached Śrīla Prabhupāda offering toilet paper [11]. But toilet paper and cigarettes are not the same. Toilet paper is not sinful; a cigarette is a forbidden, intoxicating item. So does the comparison still hold?

Yes, he will benefit! The physical item he offered is a forbidden, sinful item, and the action itself is in the mode of ignorance. But what is his intention? His intention is to give something valuable to a devotee of Kṛṣṇa. His intention is good! The simple desire to give is so auspicious and wonderful that Kṛṣṇa notes it immediately. Kṛṣṇa responds to our internal desire to serve, not just our external, mechanical activities. He sees what we are keeping behind; He doesn't see what we are giving Him.

Think about it practically: before you call a person, or send them a message, you are already thinking of them. You cannot call someone without first thinking of them—the intention always comes before the action. Kṛṣṇa looks at that intention, which comes first, not merely at the finished action.

---

## **The One-Rupee Receipt**

The moment pride, motivation, or business calculation enters our giving, it is instantly contaminated by the mode of passion.

One day, a person came and gave me a donation of exactly one rupee. One rupee! But do you know what he demanded next? He said: *“Give me a receipt.”*

The receipt itself was more expensive than the one rupee he had donated! But he insisted: *“Give me a receipt. I want to show it to my wife so she knows I am a very charitable man!”*

Look at that motivated mentality! This is charity in the mode of passion. People try to play business with Kṛṣṇa, using a tiny donation to purchase a grand reputation to show off before their own family. It is not pure; it is contaminated with selfish desire.

---

## **The Stingy Lady and the Dry Palace**

In the *Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa*, there is a historical narration of a lady who was extremely stingy [12]. She had never given a single thing in charity in her entire life.

One day, a sādhu came to her door to beg. She did not want to give him anything, but because he was standing there, she reluctantly took a lump of dirt from the ground and threw it into the begging bowl of the sādhu. The sādhu quietly took it and left.

Look at the extraordinary law of karma: because she had the desire to give *something* to a sādhu—even if it was just dirt—she was promoted in her next life. She was born into a grand palace. But when she entered it, **there was absolutely no food inside!** It was a magnificent, empty palace without any facilities.

She inquired: *“I have been given this nice palace, but why are there no facilities inside?”*

She was told: *“Because of the offense you committed. That is why you have a palace without facilities.”*

She was then instructed to perform a wonderful act of atonement to counteract her past offense: a period of fasting and penance. When she completed it with sincere devotion, her heart was purified, her offenses were dissolved, and her palace became full of auspicious facilities. The desire to give is important, but we must learn how to give properly, with a respectful and clean heart.

---

## **Paid Pujaris and the Corporate Mentality**

Whenever some personal expectation of salary or commercial negotiation enters our service, it is immediately contaminated by passion and business calculations.

I have seen devotees suffering in this way. They are wearing saffron robes, they are performing Deity worship, but they are *paid*. I am not speaking about outside people; I am speaking about inside our own movement. They are performing Deity worship for a salary!

Is that devotional service? No, it is not devotional service—it is business! They are doing business with Kṛṣṇa!

I visited one temple, and when I walked in, I asked: *“What is the energy here? Why does this temple feel like a corporate office? The whole atmosphere looks like a company.”*

I was informed that almost everyone serving there was paid, including the temple president! That corporate, commercial energy was so prominent that it pervaded the entire temple area. 

When you perform your service for a salary, the consciousness is completely different. The moment salary enters, business consciousness takes over. And do you know what happens to these paid pujaris? Some of them use this shocking, horrific phrase: **“Deity worship is boring.”**

Is Kṛṣṇa boring? It is true—it is true! For them, it has become true.

It is boring to them because there is no reciprocation between the Deities and the pujari. And why is there no reciprocation? Because there is no love! When a person is offensive and treats Kṛṣṇa like a business transaction, Kṛṣṇa simply turns His face away. It becomes an abomination. Therefore, their service becomes boring, it becomes fatigue, and it feels like punishment.

A pujari must communicate with Kṛṣṇa because the Deities are not wood, they are not stones, and they are not marble. They are directly the Supreme Personality of Godhead! We should never serve Him with a grudge. In whatever way we can, we must serve Him with gratitude.

Let us be very clear: if a devotee is working in the temple because they want to dedicate their life, and due to unavoidable family responsibilities the temple management decides to support them with a maintenance allowance, that is different. But that suggestion should never come from the devotee! It must come from the temple management.

The temple management should see the devotee's utility and invite them: *“Please, we want you to focus on this service, and we will support your family’s maintenance so you don't have to work outside.”* That is pure and auspicious. But the moment the devotee starts demanding and bargaining: *“Gurudev, this maintenance is too small for me now. I cannot manage my house. Please increase my salary,”* that is pure business, and it completely blocks our spiritual advancement.

---

## **“Sweet Heart” Prasādam**

Let us look at another area where we have introduced this offensive, business-like consciousness: the way we distribute Kṛṣṇa’s prasādam.

But before we look at how we treat the receivers, let us also be honest about how we treat those who are asked to give. One time, we went to meet some of our life members and requested a donation, explaining our need fully. The man listened carefully, and then he said: *“This is too much for charity. Charity cannot be like this—charity should be something small.”* This is the very mentality we have toward beggars and toward genuine need: we quietly decide, in advance, how small charity is allowed to be, so that it never has to meet the actual need in front of us.

In some of our temples, especially in India, I have seen devotees standing at the prasādam stalls shouting at and insulting the people who come! They yell: *“Come down here! Take!”*

Is that charity? Insulting a human being before you hand them Kṛṣṇa’s mercy? And what is that prasādam we are giving with so much shouting? Not even a full plate—just a small scoop of *khichdi*!

*Khichdi* is a poor man’s food, and we distribute it in a very useless way, making the recipient feel useless. That is not charity! Charity must be given from a **sweet heart**, so that the person who receives it feels uplifted.

Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī explains that the purpose of giving a gift (*dadāti*) is to develop spiritual love [6]. The physical object you give is not the real thing—the object is merely the medium. The real substance is the heart’s desire to make the other person happy, which is transferred through that object. When you help someone in a time of great need from a sweet, loving heart, they can never forget you, and genuine spiritual love is developed.

---

## **The Guru’s Assets and Yamarāja's Warning**

Kṛṣṇa warns that not even the government—not the prime minister, not the president—has any right over a Brahmaṇa's property. No one has a right over it. Simply to look at a Brahmaṇa's property with envious interest is enough of an offense to send three generations of your family to hell [9].

We must understand where we are and what we are doing. Otherwise, we are putting ourselves and our families in great danger. If we become devotees, our family members are liberated. But if we commit offenses and play business games with Kṛṣṇa’s money, our family members will go to hell alongside us!

Now suppose you sincerely give a donation for your Guru, trusting the person who is meant to deliver it to him—but that person misuses it, keeping it for himself instead. Does your charity still count? Yes! You gave with a pure heart, trusting your Guru's servant to do his duty; that is not your fault, and you still receive the full benefit. But the one who misused it does not benefit at all—the fault, and the reaction, are entirely his.

But if a person wears the neck beads (*kaṇṭhī-mālā*) and then takes or misuses Kṛṣṇa’s money and the Guru’s assets for his own personal benefit, **Yamarāja will personally remove his neck beads!**

Yamarāja has strictly ordered his servants: *“Those persons who are wearing the kaṇṭhī-mālā, don’t go near them. Never arrest them.”* But if someone wears the neck beads and plays business games with Kṛṣṇa's and the Guru's assets—using the Guru's money to build his own personal house—Yamarāja says: *“My servants will remove his neck beads, arrest him, and take him straight to hell, where he will pay severely!”*

For a minor offense, Kṛṣṇa may forgive you once. But if you continue to cheat Him, Kṛṣṇa will personally use His **Sudarśana Cakra** to punish you! Never play business games with the Guru’s assets.

---

## **The Passion of Separatist Devotion**

When we perform our spiritual practices or give charity with some subtle expectation—even the desire to go back to Godhead—that is still contaminated by the mode of passion.

In his teachings to Devahūti, Kapiladeva explains that devotional service performed with some expectation is charity in the mode of passion—the person is operating as a **separatist** [7]. Their interest and Kṛṣṇa's interest are separate.

This is why Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, in the prayer *ahaituky apratihatā*, insisted that devotional service must be **causeless**—*ahaituki*, without any clause, demand, or expectation attached. The moment personal interest enters, it is contaminated; the service becomes obstructed, and its result is automatically blocked. As it is prayed in the verse *mama janmani janmani* [8], one may begin with an expectation—“I want to go back to Godhead”—but at the stage of purity, even that expectation falls away.

In the beginning, we may have expectations. We tell a child: *“Drink this bitter medicine, and I will give you a sweet candy.”* The child drinks the medicine strictly to get the sweet. But when we become spiritual adults, we should know that we must take the medicine of *bhakti* without demanding any sweet candies from Kṛṣṇa.

Look at Dhruva Mahārāja [10]. In the beginning, he performed austerities because he wanted a kingdom greater than his great-grandfather Brahmā's. But when Kṛṣṇa personally appeared before him, he could only say: *“I am very sorry. Asking You for something was actually a great offense. Please forgive me, and do not give it to me again.”* That is pure, non-calculating devotion.

---

## **Personal Giving vs. QR Codes**

Today, we are living in a modern world where we think we are very advanced, very smart, and very fast. We perform our charity through online transactions, bank transfers, and QR codes.

You can give through electronic transactions. No problem, you can support the temple like that. But let us understand: **the spiritual feeling is not the same.**

That is **impersonalism**. 

Personalism is completely different. It is based on active reciprocation and spiritual feelings. When you physically come to the temple, you see the beautiful Deities, you associate with the devotees, you give your donation personally from your hand, and you take prasādam. That personal, physical association is what purifies us.

If you do everything alone through a screen, how will you get the purification? We are missing so many deep, spiritual things in the name of being very smart and fast. It is far better if we come to the temple and give personally.

And this personal exchange is not limited to formal donations. If you give a devotee a small gift—simply to support and encourage them—that is charity too. Even something as ordinary as a bag or a book, given to help another devotee, carries the same spiritual weight as a temple donation, because it is done person to person, from the heart. This is why it is so good to practice this personally with each other: it is a simple exchange, but it is very, very powerful, and it lets the feeling of love actually pass between giver and receiver.

Let us throw away our dry, corporate calculations. Give from a sweet heart, without expecting anything in return, and become a simple, non-calculating servant of the Guru.

## **Chapter 22: Heart Check**

### **My Intentions in Giving**
Before you make any donation or perform any service for the temple or the devotees, look into your heart:

*   Am I giving to get something in return—like prestige, respect, a salary, or a tax receipt to show others how charitable I am?
*   *Realize:* Kṛṣṇa responds to our intention, not just our material activities. He sees what we are keeping behind; He doesn't see what we are giving Him.
*   *A diagnostic:* If you find yourself working very hard and getting very little, or working hard and feeling constant anxiety, take it as a sign—your intention is not correct. Kṛṣṇa is responding to what is actually behind your effort, not just the effort itself.

### **The Pujari’s Communication**

*   Do you perform your Deity worship, your Japa, or your daily temple duties mechanically, like a paid worker, or do you actively communicate with Kṛṣṇa with gratitude in your heart?
*   *Commitment:* Next time you stand before the Deities, put away any grudge. Look at the Lord, feel His living presence, and pray to Him from the core of your heart.

### **Distributing the Mercy**

*   When you serve or distribute prasādam to others, do you treat them with a "sweet heart," or do you shout at them and make them feel useless?
*   *The Standard:* Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī explains that the physical object is just the means; the real gift is the heart's desire to make the other person happy.
