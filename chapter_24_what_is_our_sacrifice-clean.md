# Chapter 24: What is Our Sacrifice?

I remember in 2004, I was staying at the Radha Gopinath Temple in Chowpatty, Mumbai. His Holiness Radhanath Swami observed the same thing: the children of our congregation were spending far more time in the wrong, materialistic association than in good, spiritual association. 

Maharaj asked the parents directly: **“Do you want to leave your children like this?”** 

They said, *“No.”* 

So, with just four students, they started a small school called Gopal Garden [1]. In the beginning, there was no building, no big facilities, nothing. But by the time I visited, that humble initiative had grown into a big, well-established building where children were being trained in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. 

I had a Christian background before I came to Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The Christians understand the power of education; they have their own schools, and now they have their own universities in Nigeria as well.

But why are “Kṛṣṇa’s children” not becoming devotees? 

Have we checked why? Ask any devotee honestly, and the answer is the same: we do not have our own educational system. We have simply not prioritized the spiritual formation of the next generation. 

Śrīla Prabhupāda bluntly called these modern secular schools **“slaughterhouses”** [2]. Just as innocent animals are sent to slaughterhouses to have their bodies killed, we send our innocent children to these materialistic schools to have their spiritual consciousness systematically slaughtered. 

If we want to save our children, we must make particular, serious arrangements for their spiritual education. Sending them to the temple program for two hours on Sunday while leaving them for six hours a day in a secular school is a complete waste of labor. It is exactly like trying to **use a basket to fetch water**! 

If you try to carry water in a wicker basket, what will happen? The water will leak out completely! Only a mystic like Durvāsā Muni can use a wicker basket to carry water without it leaking. But let me ask you: how many Durvāsā Munis are sitting in our temple rooms today? None of us! So why are you trying to carry the spiritual consciousness of your children in the wicker basket of secular education? Settle this in your mind: we must take responsibility for our children's spiritual formation and build our own schools.

---

## **Overcoming Selfishness and Prioritizing the Future**

Let me say something very heavy and very direct: **devotees are extremely, deeply selfish.** 

We make grand, lavish plans for weddings. We design highly elaborate, static, and luxurious wedding ceremonies that last for just one day of prestige. 

But what is our plan for the children that come after that marriage? What is our plan for their spiritual future? Zero! We have no plan. Is that selflessness or is that deep selfishness? 

We are constructing big temples, but where are the schools? If you can build a grand temple, why can you not build a school? 

In fact, you will get far more money and support for a school than a temple! Many philanthropic and secular organizations will happily donate to build a school because they see the immediate benefit it brings to society. But because we are selfish and short-sighted, we hesitate. 

I am sorry to say this, but **we have leaders without vision.** 

Our GBC and temple leaders do not look at the importance of our children, and they are not planning for our future. They think only about their own bellies, their personal positions, and their prestige. Their philosophy is not clean because it is constantly controlled by politics. 

If you are not looking ahead to who is going to maintain this mission tomorrow, you are not a leader. 

Before I came to India, I sat down with all the devotees in Nigeria and challenged them: **“Look at my body. My body is old. If I give this society to you today, which of you is ready to handle it?”** 

If you have built a million temples and initiated a billion disciples, but not a single one of them is spiritually qualified to represent you and carry on the pure lineage after you leave—you are an absolute, total failure!

---

## **Śrīla Prabhupāda’s Standard of Boiling the Milk**

What is real success in Kṛṣṇa consciousness? 

Success is not measured by how many congregation members we have, or how many grand facilities we own. Success is: **How many of us are actually becoming pure devotees?** 

Śrīla Prabhupāda famously gave us a crucial instruction: **“Boil the milk.”** [3]

We are very expert at getting the milk—preaching, distributing books, attracting people, and building new facilities. But are we boiling the milk to make it thick and sweet? Are we developing devotees, character, depth, and genuine qualifications?

If you do not boil the milk, it will spoil and become useless. We must not be satisfied simply with bringing new people; we must be satisfied with keeping the devotees and refining their character. 

Think about how Kṛṣṇa Himself would see this. When our time comes to finally go back home, back to Godhead, what happens to everyone we leave behind? Kṛṣṇa does not say, "Never mind, come alone, leave those fools here." Kṛṣṇa asks: **“How many people are you bringing with you? How many?”** If you were once praised for cleaning the temple nicely, His next instruction is never "good, now you are finished"—it is "teach that to someone else too." If you are collecting nicely, do not simply collect for yourself; show others how you are collecting, so that all of us collect together. That is our system—selflessness, not personal credit.

Prabhupāda clearly made his intentions known: *“If I can make even one pure devotee, I will consider my entire attempt a success.”* 

He designed everything in ISKCON—from college preaching and Life Membership to food for life, restaurants, farm communities, and Deity worship—for that single, unadulterated purpose: to make pure devotees. *“An ācārya should devise a means by which people may somehow or other come to Kṛṣṇa consciousness,”* he taught [7]. He spoke it, he lived it, and he gave it to us. He was completely purpose-driven. 

Because he was purpose-driven, he could tackle all the obstacles and successfully establish this movement. If we want to please him, we must boil the milk.

---

## **Establishing Self-Sufficient Devotee Communities**

A true devotee-centered future cannot exist only inside concrete city buildings. We need self-sufficient communities. 

A community is not just big buildings everywhere. A real community means: **the school is there, the farm is there, the cows are there, and the agricultural lands are there.** [4] This is exactly the vision my spiritual master, His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Mahārāja, gave his life to establish.

I have never read anywhere in the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* that Kṛṣṇa fed His cows dry grass inside a concrete house. Kṛṣṇa took the cows out to graze. He walked with them in the forests of Vraja, and they ate fresh, green grass. We must return to this natural, Vedic lifestyle where schools, agriculture, and cow protection are integrated together. 

Bhakti Tīrtha Mahārāja emphasized this so much in West Africa. He started it small, seeing the future, wanting us to be self-sufficient and not look for anything from the outside. He demonstrated cottage industries, schools, and clinics. Today in West Africa, we have two schools—one in Nigeria (Port Harcourt) and one in Ghana—where even non-devotees send their children to be trained. They learn everything Kṛṣṇa consciously!

But not everyone follows this vision. Before coming here this time, I went to see some other projects our own leaders are building elsewhere. Buildings, buildings, buildings—everywhere. But not a single plant, not one seed of grain. No farming at all. We eat grains every day, yet nobody is planning to grow them; instead there are tourist centers, when what actually sustains us is food from the land.

In Nigeria, we have acquired one hundred acres of land, and in Ghana, we have two hundred and fifty acres. We are not building these farms for material business; we are developing the Surabhi project as a direct, loving offering to him because that is what he wanted. 

A genuine disciple’s only duty is to fulfill the desires of the spiritual master. We are pushing these schools and farms with all our energy to show the world that Kṛṣṇa is always present when His devotees cooperate with selflessness.

---

## **Making Sincere Sacrifices for the Preaching Mission**

Devotees often tell me that although every parent wants this, there is always some hesitation, some fear, about actually coming forward to start it. 

Why are you afraid? Fear of what? 

Are you afraid because Kṛṣṇa is not there? Kṛṣṇa is always there! 

If you are not ready to face challenges, you will never make anything wonderful happen in this world. You will remain babies forever! 

Look at how our previous Ācāryas struggled to give us this sublime path.

* **Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya** was persecuted so severely by the Shaivite king that they wanted to kill him [5]. He had to flee his home in Srirangam and run to Melkote. The king’s soldiers arrested and brutally tortured his disciples, blinding Kureśa and Mahāpūrṇa; only one managed to escape. 
* **Śrīla Prabhupāda** made many attempts, underwent unimaginable struggles, and made the ultimate sacrifice to give us this society. 

They made these extreme, life-threatening sacrifices, and today we are sitting comfortably in these beautifully decorated rooms enjoying the fruits of their labor. 

But let me ask you a very direct question: **What is our own sacrifice?** 

What are you risking for Kṛṣṇa? Sincere sacrifice is our turn to give back, and it must go beyond just schools. What time, money, comfort, prestige, career opportunity, energy, or ambition are we willing to sacrifice for the devotees who will come after us? Sincere schools and farms are just our major examples.

And building the school is not even the end of it—we must protect its purpose once it exists. I have seen a school where so many outside, non-devotee children were admitted that their materialistic mentality began to spoil the whole atmosphere for everyone. It made no sense. You cannot go to a Muslim school and teach the *Gītā* there; if you go there, you have to learn the Koran. So if we bring people into our own school and still fail to teach them the *Gītā*, what have we accomplished? We must transform the students we bring in, not let their mentality transform our school.

Sometimes, devotees think with a very narrow, business mind: *“Maharaj, why should I spend my hard-earned money and time to build a school? By the time the school is finished, my own children will have already graduated. What will I get out of it?”* 

If you think with this business calculation, **you are not a devotee!** 

A devotee never asks, *“What will I get out of it?”* A devotee only asks, *“What can I give?”*

Think of the person who discovered electricity. He did not sit back and think, *“Oh, if I invent this, I will not personally get to enjoy the light bulb before I die.”* He simply wanted to help humanity, and because of his selflessness, we are all sitting under bright lights today. 

We must have that exact same spirit of selflessness. We must make sacrifices today so that the devotees of tomorrow have a safe harbor to protect their souls and continue the disciplic succession after we are gone.

Śrīla Prabhupāda's expectation was that the third generation of our children should be so pure that they can **light a fire with mantras**! This is his expectation. That will come from pure **Garbhādhāna-saṁskāra** and careful training [6]. Samskara means to prepare the consciousness, making everything auspicious for a divine person to appear. These children did not succeed in their own spiritual life before; Kṛṣṇa is giving them a rare chance to become successful this time, by placing them in a devotee family. Prabhupāda was teaching us how important our children are—he even told a disciple once: *“Leave Deity worship, go and take care of your child.”*

This is not a small thing we are being asked to protect—it is nothing less than the future of the disciplic succession itself. Our whole journey together has been a steady, progressive ascent of the soul. In the beginning, we learned that spiritual life begins only when we are willing to put our heads on the ground in full, unreserved surrender at the feet of the Guru. We then learned how to wire our hearts directly to the powerhouse of his instructions, hearing him with undivided attention and serving his Vāṇī even in separation. And now, in this final hour, we are called to make the ultimate, risk-taking sacrifice to protect the future of his mission and preserve the next generation of devotees.

So let us come together, sit down on the floor, and discuss what to do. Let us stop acting like babies, drop our selfish prestige, and make a glorious, risk-taking offering of sacrifice at the feet of Śrīla Prabhupāda and our spiritual masters! This is where our journey back to Godhead is completed—not in some proud, independent achievement, but by returning directly to where we first began: with our heads on the ground, fully surrendered to the dust of their lotus feet.
