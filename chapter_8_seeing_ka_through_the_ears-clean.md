# Chapter 8: Seeing Kṛṣṇa Through the Ears


How do we deepen our connection with Kṛṣṇa?

There is a beautiful, defining teaching in *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (1.8.36) given by Queen Kuntī:

> *śṛṇvanti gāyanti gṛṇanty abhīkṣṇaśaḥ*
> *smaranti nandanti tavehitaṁ janāḥ*
> *ta eva paśyanty acireṇa tāvakaṁ*
> *bhava-pravāhoparamaṁ padāmbujam*

*“O Kṛṣṇa, those who continuously hear, chant, and repeat Your transcendental activities, or take pleasure in others doing so, certainly see Your lotus feet, which alone can stop the repetition of birth and death.”*[1]

Look carefully at this verse, and you will see that it is not simply a prayer. It is a teaching. Queen Kuntī is instructing us directly.

Please listen carefully to this word she uses: *abhīkṣṇaśaḥ*—continuously. She says we must hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, non-stop. 

Why continuously? Because hearing about Kṛṣṇa is not a ritual we complete and then move on from—it is not like a class in a material school, where the day you graduate, your connection with the professor is finished. Our connection with Kṛṣṇa is eternal. We are not preparing for something that will end; we are preparing right now for the very service we will render Him eternally, once we are perfected. So we must never let this become a ritual, a ceremony we simply perform for a while and then set aside.

This process of hearing did not begin with us, and it will not end with us. It has been going on eternally. We are simply meeting it on our way, and by good fortune, we are meeting it now.

Why? Because our material eyes are completely blind. 

Even if the Lord is standing right in front of our eyes, that does not mean we can see Him. This necessary vision is not something we already possess. It has to be developed—and it is developed only through devotional service, beginning with hearing about the Lord from a genuine source. We are trying to see Kṛṣṇa before we have learned how to *hear* Him.

---

## **The Proof of Real Hearing**

This kind of hearing has real proof. Rukmiṇī Devī heard about Kṛṣṇa only once, with such attention, that He became locked in her heart eternally.[2] Mahārāja Parīkṣit had only seven days left to live, and in those seven days, hearing *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* from Śukadeva Gosvāmī without a single lapse of attention, he did not forget a thing.[3] Afterward, he was able to repeat its very essence back to his own mother, Uttarā, without a single mistake.[4] That is hearing. That is what happens when the mind is truly listening.

And hearing means the mind is listening, not just the ear. If the mind listens to Kṛṣṇa, our material entanglement is destroyed. But we complain: *“I am chanting, and my mind is disturbed.”* The truth is simpler, and less comfortable: our mind has not been chanting with us at all. When the mind actually starts to chant with us—when the mind itself is hearing—that is what destroys our material situation completely.

If we do not hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, we will end up hearing about māyā instead. There is no third, neutral option. Even if we think we are sitting on the fence, uncommitted, we are not actually outside the material world by doing so—we are simply losing on both sides. We must belong somewhere: either engaged in this world for sense gratification, or in the spiritual world for transcendental enjoyment. For that, we must hear.

And this hearing should make us glad to see others hearing too, in whatever form devotion takes them. Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura envisioned exactly this: a day when the offensive, quarrelsome portions of our different traditions are worn away, and people of all backgrounds spontaneously chant the Holy Names together, as brothers, hating no one.[5] If the whole world had this vision, nobody would fight with anybody, over anything. We would become the brothers and sisters we are actually meant to be.

---

## **Sincere Hearing vs. Physical Proximity**

We must understand that physical proximity to God does not guarantee spiritual vision. 

When Lord Kṛṣṇa was personally present on this earth, walking, talking, and performing His pastimes, did everyone see Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead? No! 

Materialists like Rāvaṇa, Kaṁsa, Hiraṇyakaśipu, Jarāsandha, and Śiśupāla were highly qualified personalities. They had immense wealth, political power, and high social standing. But they were completely blind to the presence of the Lord. 

Rāvaṇa saw Lord Rāmacandra physically. But how did he see Him? He saw Him as a weak, unfortunate prince who was crying because his wife had been stolen. Rāvaṇa thought, *“I will easily kill this helpless man.”*[6] 

Kaṁsa saw Kṛṣṇa every day in his mind, but he saw Him only as a mortal enemy—someone to kill before he himself was killed.[7] 

Jarāsandha saw Kṛṣṇa face-to-face and fought Him seventeen times. But his vision was blocked by the thick, heavy curtain of material desire. He saw Kṛṣṇa only as a cowherd boy, a runaway, and a mortal foe whom he wanted to destroy.[8] 

Śiśupāla saw Kṛṣṇa too, and his vision was calculating rather than blind with rage. Kṛṣṇa had promised that He would tolerate exactly one hundred insults from Śiśupāla before finally killing him. So every time Śiśupāla saw Kṛṣṇa, he would insult Him ninety-nine times, stop just short of the hundredth, and walk away to continue another day. That was his relationship with the Supreme Personality of Godhead standing right in front of him: a running tally, not a vision.[9]

Even Duryodhana had Kṛṣṇa personally come to him. Before the great war, Kṛṣṇa Himself went to negotiate peace. Duryodhana's response was to order that Kṛṣṇa be killed. He came to help you, and you want to kill him? He could not see Kṛṣṇa as a well-wisher of all living entities—only as an obstacle to be removed—because he, too, was blind.[10]

Hiraṇyakaśipu performed austerities so extreme that nobody else could perform them—austerity fierce enough, in principle, to obtain the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself. But because his heart was full of envy and pride, he did not want Kṛṣṇa; he wanted to dethrone Brahmā and kill Viṣṇu. What did he get at the end? He did not get Kṛṣṇa; he got death.[11] 

By contrast, what austerity did the child Prahlāda Mahārāja perform? None at all. He simply heard. He sat and heard about Kṛṣṇa with complete faith from his spiritual master, Nārada Muni. And because he heard with his mind, he got Kṛṣṇa. He got protection and mercy. This is the power of hearing.[11]

Śrīla Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura explains exactly why, citing a verse from the *Padma Purāṇa*: as a blind person cannot see anything—even with the help of a lamp or a mirror—those who are averse to Viṣṇu cannot see Him, even if He is standing right in front of them.[12]

We cannot force our way into Kṛṣṇa’s presence using our material qualifications, our wealth, or our academic degrees. True spiritual vision is not a physical adjustment; it is a gift that is granted only when our hearts are purified. And that purification can only come when we learn to sit down submissively and hear from the right sources.

---

## **Hearing from the Right Source**

And the source matters immensely. Generally, people hear from unauthorized persons—persons who may be very qualified academically, but who have not actually followed the process themselves. Hearing from such people becomes a sheer waste of time. Sometimes they even interpret the scriptures passionately, to suit their own purposes. So first, we must select a speaker who is genuinely competent and bona fide, and only then sit down to hear.

Consider, even, how we came to have Kṛṣṇa's pastimes to hear in the first place. He performed them, but not for the sake of leaving us a written record. It was Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu who came again and specifically empowered this transmission: He invested Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī with the task of writing down Kṛṣṇa's most intimate pastimes, and then empowered Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī to teach them to the entire world. That is not an accident of history. It is why we can trust what we hear, when we hear it from a line that actually goes back to the source.

But what about our own disqualification? Here is the astonishing part. If you don't know how to cook, and you burn the food, will it become tasty? No. It is burnt; nobody will eat it. You cannot do anything material wrongly and still succeed. But the holy name of Kṛṣṇa is different—it can be chanted with all of our offenses, and from those very offenses, one still becomes purified. With all our disqualifications, if we hear with faith, that faith itself gives us the qualification to actually participate—not to hear as an outsider, but to hear as one who belongs.

Gold and iron look the same to someone who has never handled either. But the difference between them is not in their appearance—it is in their nature. In exactly the same way, Kṛṣṇa's activities look, on the surface, like anyone else's activities: eating, playing, fighting, loving. But they are one hundred percent different, because they are transcendental. No one hears about the doings of an ordinary conditioned soul and becomes purified by it. But everyone who hears about the doings of Kṛṣṇa, however ordinary they may sound, becomes purified—because what they are actually hearing does not belong to this material world at all.

---

## **The Raging River and Sincere Hearing**

To understand the difference between mechanical ritual and genuine, attentive hearing, our ācāryas share a powerful history.

There were once two ladies who went together, every day for seven days, to hear a great sage speak on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. They sat side-by-side in the same audience, listening to the same speaker.[13] 

For seven days, the speaker repeatedly emphasized a single, powerful instruction: **“This material existence is an ocean, but you can easily cross it simply by chanting ‘Govinda, Govinda.’”**

At the end of the seven days, the two ladies began their journey back to their homes. On the way, they came to a wide river. While they had been sitting in the kathā, a heavy rain had fallen, and the river had become full and dangerous to cross. 

The first lady looked at the turbulent water and remembered the words of the speaker. She thought: *“Our spiritual guide told us that by chanting ‘Govinda,’ we can easily cross the entire ocean of material birth and death. If we can cross the ocean, then what is this tiny, insignificant river? It is nothing!”* 

With absolute, unwavering faith, she stepped straight onto the water, chanting **“Govinda, Govinda!”** Miraculously, she did not sink. She walked cleanly across the surface of the river and reached the other side.

The second lady, seeing this, jumped into the river as well. But the moment her feet touched the water, she began to sink and drown. 

As she was going under, she screamed desperately to her friend on the bank: *“Help me! Please, save me! Come and pull me out!”*

The first lady called back: *“Don’t you remember what the speaker told us? Why don’t you chant Govinda?”* But the drowning lady could not chant. She fumbled, choked, and drowned in the river.

Why did this happen? They sat in the same class, heard the same speaker, and went to the same temple. But for the second lady, she did not hear. Her body was in the class, but her mind was completely somewhere else. 

When her friends asked her, *“Oh, how was the lecture today?”* she would smile nicely and say, *“Oh, the speaker was so wonderful! He spoke the best lecture I have ever had!”* But if they asked her, *“Can you share one point that he made?”* she had nothing to say. She couldn’t remember a single thing. 

She did not hear. Because she did not hear, she could not remember. Because she could not remember, she had no faith. And because she had no faith, when the crisis came, she could not act. She drowned. 

Why did she fail while her friend succeeded, when both of them sat through the same seven days? Because the second lady never let it become anything more than a ritual—something you attend, not something that penetrates you. We should never let Kṛṣṇa consciousness become that kind of ritual. It is meant to be a dynamic, practical process—one that lets us see Kṛṣṇa Himself, face to face, not just hear about Him from a distance.

---

## **The Sweet Flower Handcuffs of Vṛndāvana**

Why does Kṛṣṇa demand this deep, attentive hearing? Because He is not conquered by scholarship or grand shows of formality. He is conquered only by spontaneous, informal love. 

Let us look at the sweet, playful nature of this love. Look at a beautiful, joyful pastime of Vṛndāvana.

Kṛṣṇa and the cowherd boys fight with each other every single day. But how can you fight without an enemical attitude? You punch a friend, does that make him your enemy? Their fighting is not our fighting. When we fight, we truly want to hurt one another—that is real enmity. But when Kṛṣṇa and the boys fight, they are fighting only to give Kṛṣṇa pleasure. It looks the same on the outside, but it is a completely different thing. That is the spirit of the pastime you are about to hear.

One day, Lord Kṛṣṇa was sitting in one corner of the forest, and His cowherd friends, the gopas, put Him on a mock throne. They worshiped Him, and after the worship, Kṛṣṇa looked at them and said: *“Now that I am the king, you must go and announce it. Anyone who disobeys, arrest them and bring them to the king for punishment!”*

The cowherd boys were very excited. Madhu Maṅgala, His dear brāhmaṇa friend, was the first person to start jumping here and there, shouting loudly: *“Kṛṣṇa is the king! If you see anyone picking flowers in this forest, arrest the person and take them to the king for punishment!”*

As they were marching through the forest making this announcement, they met Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī and the gopīs. The gopīs looked at the boys and said: *“What did you say? Kṛṣṇa is the king? No! Rādhārāṇī is the queen of this forest!”*

Madhu Maṅgala stepped forward, waved his hand, and argued: *“No! You can’t say that! Kṛṣṇa is the king!”*

The gopīs looked at each other and said: *“We have to deal with this boy seriously.”* 

Suddenly, the gopīs descended on the cowherd boys. The boys were completely outnumbered. Seeing the fierce determination of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī’s party, all the boys ran away—but Madhu Maṅgala was caught! He was arrested and thrown to the ground. 

The gopīs looked down at him and demanded: *“Now you tell us. Who is the king here?”*

Madhu Maṅgala looked at their faces, realized his friends had abandoned him, and quickly changed his side: *“Oh, actually... I think Kṛṣṇa is your queen!”*

The gopīs laughed and said: *“For the heavy offense you made of declaring Kṛṣṇa the king, we are going to handcuff you!”*

Now, what were their handcuffs made of? In this material world, handcuffs are made of cold, hard iron. But in Vṛndāvana, the gopīs handcuffed Madhu Maṅgala with beautiful, fragrant, soft flowers! They chained his hands tightly with garlands of sweet blossoms.

Madhu Maṅgala looked at his flower handcuffs and said: *“Well, handcuffing me is one thing. But for my next punishment... I want you to give me laddus!”* 

Since he was a “laddu brāhmaṇa” who didn’t know how to chant any Sanskrit mantras, the gopīs took great pleasure in executing this punishment. They loaded Madhu Maṅgala’s pockets on both sides with big, sweet laddus, and stuffed his mouth so full of laddus that he couldn’t even speak! 

Then they told him: *“Now go back to your friend and bring him here to declare that our queen is the supreme head of this forest.”*

Madhu Maṅgala waddled back to Kṛṣṇa, who was still sitting on his mock throne. 

Kṛṣṇa looked at him, barely recognizing him, and asked: *“Hey, my friend, what happened to you?”*

Madhu Maṅgala mumbled through his mouthful of laddus: *“What happened to me? You are asking me? This is the result of declaring you the king! I am handcuffed like this! They beat me and pulled me seriously! They caught me completely! The only way you can save yourself is to come with me right now and surrender. Otherwise, all of them are coming here, and you will see what they will do to you!”*

Madhu Maṅgala took Kṛṣṇa by the hand, dragged Him from the throne, and took Him straight to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī’s assembly. 

When they arrived, the gopīs looked at them and demanded: *“Kṛṣṇa, tell us. Who is the queen of this forest?”*

Kṛṣṇa looked at Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, bowed His head, and said: *“Rādhārāṇī is the queen.”*

Immediately, the gopīs picked up Kṛṣṇa, sat Him on the same throne next to Rādhārāṇī, and worshiped them both together with great love. 

**This is the feast for the soul.** 

We take prasāda for our physical bodies every day. But hearing these sweet, playful, and intimate activities of the Lord is the true feast for the soul. When we hear with faith, we are handcuffed not by the iron chains of material karma, but by the sweet flower handcuffs of Kṛṣṇa’s love.

Let us stop being superficial, mechanical ritualists. Let us put away our academic arguments, sit submissively on the floor, and hear with our whole hearts. That is the only way our eyes will finally be opened to see the beautiful lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa.

## **Chapter 8: Heart Check**

### **Am I Actually Hearing?**
Think honestly about your recent classes, reading sessions, or daily chanting:

* Am I sitting physically present while my mind is completely somewhere else, scrolling through anxieties, duties, and plans? 
* Did I walk away from the last Bhāgavatam lecture with a specific point, instruction, or pastime planted in my heart, or did I simply think *“that was a nice class”* while remembering nothing?
* *Realize:* She did not hear. If we cannot remember what we hear, we cannot build faith, and when the turbulent rivers of material crisis rise, we will drown.

### **Cleansing Our Blocks to Hearing**
Our vision to see Kṛṣṇa is not something we are born with. It has to be developed, and it develops only through devotional service, beginning with sincere hearing from a genuine source.

* Am I trying to see Kṛṣṇa before I have honestly put in the work of hearing about Him?
* Am I hearing from a source I actually trust to be genuine and bona fide, or simply from whoever happens to be convenient?
* *Realize:* Jarāsandha stood face-to-face with the Lord but saw only a cowherd enemy, while the child Prahlāda simply sat and heard from his Guru, and got Kṛṣṇa.

### **The Feast for the Soul**

* **Aural Reception:** Spend at least ten uninterrupted minutes this week listening to the words of Śrīla Prabhupāda or your spiritual master. Turn off all phones, close your eyes, and give it your full attention—not just your ears, but your mind. Afterward, see if you can repeat back even one specific point, in your own words.
* **The Food:** What sweet pastime or instruction will I feed my soul with today?
