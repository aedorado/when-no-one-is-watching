# Chapter 9: The Sincere and the Deviant

I remember a session where a devotee finally said what many of us think but rarely say out loud: “Mahārāja, you’ve told us this process is undoubtedly difficult—you yourself said you would never have accepted it if it were easy. But we’re also told that Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu made this process easy and gives it away freely. Which is it?”

That question deserves a real answer, not a comfortable one. Let us enter into it directly, without any cosmetics, and let the truth speak.

## **Valuing the Rare Gift of Devotion**

If Kṛṣṇa consciousness is undoubtedly very difficult, why would anyone willingly accept it?

If Kṛṣṇa consciousness were cheap, I would not follow it. I tell you this straight. Why? Because anything which is cheap has no value, and anyone can follow it. 

Look at the material world. If you go to the market, the vegetable shop is crowded, packed with people shouting and pushing. But if you walk down the street to the diamond shop, how many people do you see inside? Very few. Why? Because a diamond is costly. The value, the quality, is of a completely different grade. 

Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the ultimate diamond; it can never be a cheap process. It is difficult because when you achieve perfection in this path, your material existence is terminated for eternity, and you return back home, back to Godhead, never to return to this world of misery again. Because we have been deviant and have misbehaved in millions of ways since time immemorial, we have a massive mountain of dirt to clean. And that purification process is not cheap—it is difficult. 

So when we say Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has made the process “easy,” what do we mean? We mean that if you compare bhakti-yoga to other paths like Aṣṭāṅga-yoga, bhakti is a walk in the park. Do you have any idea what Aṣṭāṅga-yoga requires? You have to go to the forest, tie your leg to a tree, and hang upside down with your head on the ground for years and years. How many of us can do that? None of us! Compared to that brutal, agonizing physical torture, chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa is wonderfully sweet and easy. 

But make no mistake: to chant the Holy Name with pure, undivided attention is not a simple, mechanical trick. If it were easy, we would not be standing here pretending. We would have tears pouring from our eyes every single morning. 

When pure devotees saw the Deities on the altar, they did not just stand there politely checking their watches. When Śrīnivāsācārya saw Lord Govinda, he fainted on the spot, completely unconscious in ecstatic love. [3] When Sañjaya saw the peacock fan of Kṛṣṇa, he collapsed. [4]

Think of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī: when Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī gave him a copy of his book *Lalita-mādhava*, Raghunātha dāsa took the book and cried every single day just by holding it and reading its pastimes! [5] Think of Gadādhara Paṇḍita: he couldn't even read his own *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* because his eyes were so full of tears of ecstatic love that the water literally wiped out every single syllable on the page! When Śrīnivāsa Ācārya came to fetch that Bhāgavatam from him, he had to go and look for a different copy instead—because Gadādhara Paṇḍita's own copy could no longer be read. [6]

Let us look at what Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī described as his own personal experience of chanting the Holy Name. He said: *“I do not know the potency of these two syllables: Kṛṣ-ṇa. I cannot explain what is within these two syllables. When Kṛṣṇa is chanted, the eye does not see anything. The ear does not hear anything else. The tongue does not chant anything else—there is no distraction. Only Kṛṣṇa.”* [7] He was not pretending; that was his actual, unadulterated experience. He never complained about his mind wandering or being distracted. His mind was asleep to this material world, and awake only to Kṛṣṇa.

## **Protecting Our Consciousness From Sinful Desires**

Why do we look at the Deities and feel nothing? Why do we chant and feel dry?

Because our eyes are completely dry. What is drying our eyes? Offenses. Offenses dry up our eyes.

We are roommates with Kṛṣṇa, but we are not on speaking terms! Is that normal? You stay in the same room with someone and you don’t talk to them—there is something seriously wrong! We stand before Him and treat Him like a stone or wooden idol. But the Deity is not an idol. If you are dealing with the Deities, you must communicate with Him. Kṛṣṇa is personally there, but because of our offenses, our vision is blocked.

This Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are chanting has extraordinary spiritual potency. It is the exact same mantra! Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has told us that Candra, the moon god, chanted Hare Kṛṣṇa. [8] And because the moon chanted, Lord Śiva’s blue neck—which became blue from drinking the poison of the ocean—became completely white! The japa of the moon god actually removed the poison from Śiva's throat and turned it white. When Śiva went home, his wife Pārvatī was so shocked that she doubted him and said: *“Don’t come near me! You are not my husband! Go!”* She was not pretending; she was completely bewildered because her husband's neck had turned white from the chanting of the Holy Name!

And look at Lord Balarāma’s dhotī. His dhotī is eternally blue. But Nārada Muni’s japa turned Balarāma’s blue dhotī completely white! Even the Yamunā River turned completely milky because of the chanting, and the gopīs churned it and got sweet yogurt from it! [9]

This is the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are chanting. It is not a different mantra. Śrīla Prabhupāda is chanting the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, and the whole world is dancing in the kīrtana of the Holy Name. Why are we not having the same experience? Because we are criminals, deviants, and offenders since time immemorial.

Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura has already confessed this on our behalf: *“My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, there is no sin I have not committed a million times.”* [10] He is not merely speaking of himself—he is teaching us what we are. There is no sin, no offense, that we ourselves have not committed a million times over. What is it, then, that has actually kept us bound in this material world since time immemorial? Not bhakti—bhakti carries us back to Godhead. It is our sinful life that keeps us here. We have to pay the heavy price of purification before we can experience the same nectar. We must purify ourselves to get to that level.

When we are struggling on this path of purification, our minds become disturbed by the unjust, cruel, or negative behavior of others. Sincere devotees sometimes feel that when they face such trials, even performing Kṛṣṇa consciousness feels painful.

But I must tell you something very heavy: **A sincere devotee can never feel mental disturbance.** It is not possible.

If a sincere devotee feels mental disturbance and is overwhelmed by anxiety, it would mean that Kṛṣṇa is failing in His promise to protect His surrendered servants. But Kṛṣṇa never fails. He always protects His devotees.

Why do we get disturbed by the unjust behavior of others? Because we have the material contamination inside us. If I do not have lust in my body, nothing outside can make me lusty. If I do not have anger in my heart, no insult can make me angry. If I do not have envy, the competitive actions of others can never make me envious. The fire inside your heart is what connects with the fire outside. When people insult or mistreat you, they are simply throwing a spark. If your heart is full of dry, material attachments, it will instantly catch fire. But if your heart is saturated with the nectar of bhakti, that spark will immediately be extinguished.

Sincere devotees are protected by Kṛṣṇa. Let us look at a beautiful pastime in the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*. There was a poor devotee named Śrīdhara—so poor that he barely survived selling banana bark for a living—who used to cry out the holy names late into the night. [11] The blasphemers and envious atheists nearby couldn't stand it, and they started criticizing him directly: *“See this, just see this one! Look, he has no cloth on his body, he has no food to eat. The whole night he cried due to hunger, but now he wants to show that he has the love of Godhead. Just look at him!”*

They were not speaking far away from him; they were speaking right before his face. But Śrīdhara was not even there! Why? Why was he not there? Because he was fully Kṛṣṇa conscious, submerged in ecstatic love of the Lord. There was no space for anything else to enter his heart. He was completely empty of material identification, and completely full of Kṛṣṇa.

Why do we hear what people talk about us? Why do we get so disturbed, so offended, and so angry when someone criticizes us? **Because we are empty!** We are empty of Kṛṣṇa, and full of ourselves. Because we identify with this temporary body, we respond to insults negatively: *“Why did you say like that about me? Who are you to tell me that?”* But Śrīdhara didn’t say that. The great saint Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja didn’t say that. They were full of Kṛṣṇa, and there was no empty space in their hearts to receive insults. Sādhana is what fills that empty space. When we practice Sādhana nicely, we become strong, and all these material disturbances simply bounce off our chest.

## **Overcoming the Illusion of Spiritual Advancement**

When bhakti begins to work, it immediately relieves our suffering. This is the first quality of pure devotional service described by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī: *kleśaghnī*—the distress-relieving quality [1]. When we run to Kṛṣṇa consciousness from the burning fire of material life, bhakti immediately puts that fire out. Suddenly, we feel comfortable, peaceful, and relieved.

And that is exactly where the trap of Māyā lies! We feel, *“I am okay now. My mind is stable, my life is comfortable.”* And so, we become complacent. We slide into a comfortable, lazy routine where our practices feel monotonous and mechanical. We start coming to the temple only when we feel like it, treating our Sādhana like a casual hobby.

Look at what this actually does to us. Our services become a string of dull, dragging meetings we merely hang around for, suffering through them rather than relishing them. And worse: the moment a devotee starts feeling "I'm all right now," that same complacency quietly turns other devotees into servants in our own mind. We stop seeing them as fellow souls to serve and start expecting them to serve us.

In the *Bhagavad-gītā* (16.17), Kṛṣṇa uses a very heavy word for this state: *ātmasambhāvitā*—self-complacent and arrogant. [12] He says that complacency is the quality of a demon, not a devotee! We came to Kṛṣṇa consciousness to become devotees, not self-satisfied demons.

Let me tell you a story about my musician friends from my youth. They formed a band called **Cloud Seven**. There were six boys. In the beginning, they had nothing, so they put an incredible, desperate amount of effort into practicing, writing, and refining their music. When their first record came out, it was an absolute hit. They made massive amounts of money and achieved huge success.

But then, they started enjoying their success. They became complacent. When it was time to record their second album, they didn’t put the same effort into practicing. They foolishly thought, *“The effort we put into the first record will automatically carry the second one.”*

Do you know what happened? The second album made nothing—it was a total failure!

Success is a stimulant, but success can also bring us down. If we are not careful to continuously expand our devotion, complacency will destroy us. So many devotees in Kṛṣṇa consciousness are simply floating, with no real purpose. To be successful, we must be Kṛṣṇa-purpose-driven, constantly checking in on ourselves: am I really serving Kṛṣṇa? Is He satisfied with what I am offering Him? Is Kṛṣṇa actually enjoying this service, or am I just going through the motions? The principle of love is this: if what I offer to Kṛṣṇa today pleases Him, tomorrow I must expand it. Bhakti is a dynamic, moving process. The moment you try to just “maintain” your standard without any desire to grow, you have already begun to slide backward. **Maintenance is Māyā!**

No! That is maintenance, and maintenance is Māyā. We have to expand it. We must always push ourselves further.

To defeat this complacency, we must understand our priorities: **Sādhana is our primary, non-negotiable duty.** Services come *after* Sādhana. One should never even go to the altar without having chanted at least a few rounds first. Do not go and cook for the Deities if you have not chanted. Kṛṣṇa is not a hungry man—He is hungry for the love of His devotees, not for our bhoga. To actually serve Kṛṣṇa, you must complete your japa first; only then are you qualified to offer Him anything at all.



## **Avoiding Bargains and Business with the Supreme**

Often, when we face difficulties, we try to bargain with Kṛṣṇa. We pray desperately for certain material adjustments—like a job with better hours or relief from family stress—believing that these changes will make our environment more conducive for Sādhana. But when we pray and fast for years, and Kṛṣṇa does not answer, we feel neglected. Why does He ignore our sincere prayers?

There is a statement in the *Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta* that captures exactly what is going on in Kṛṣṇa's mind when we pray like this:

*“I am not a fool like this person. Let Me take My time to purify this person first, before I give him or her what he or she is asking for.”* [13]

Kṛṣṇa is not deaf! He hears everything. But when we are praying, we should check: does our karma allow us to get this now?

Let me give you an example. We have a devotee who has a very high night shift job. He is trying to get a morning job, but for a long time he is not getting it. He has been praying to Kṛṣṇa for a long time, but nothing helps. He feels he can practice better if he has a morning job. But because his karma doesn’t allow him to have what he is begging for, or because it is not right for him, Kṛṣṇa keeps quiet.

Let me give you another example. Some time back, a devotee told me—and I know him very well: *“Maharaj, if I get a car... if I get a car every day, every Maṅgala-ārati I will be there. Every Maṅgala-ārati I will be there. Please pray for me.”*

Kṛṣṇa pleased, he got a car!

For three years, I did not see him. Finally, I met him and said, *“Prabhu, how are you? Do you remember what you said? You said if you get a car, you will come for Maṅgala-ārati every day. Look, is this your own car?”*

*“My car,”* he said.

*“Then why are you not coming? For three years I have not been seeing you here with the car.”*

And do you know what he told me next? He said, *“Oh, Maharaj, if I have two cars, then I will give one to my wife, and use one to come to the temple!”*

Look at the cheating, duplicitous mentality! We think we can play smart with Kṛṣṇa!

But you can never cheat Him. Kṛṣṇa is not a “straight” man. He is extremely crooked. In the *Bhagavad-gītā* (10.36), He boldly declares: *“I am the gambling of the cheat.”* [2] He doesn’t hide it! If you try to play smart with Him, He will cheat you back so beautifully, so peacefully, and without any struggle, that you will spend your whole life struggling in confusion while He remains completely untroubled. We cannot artificially do it. Please let us not forget that we are dealing with a person who knows us completely better than we know ourselves.

Here is a simple test to check yourself. If Kṛṣṇa gives you a comfortable job, will you still come to the temple program? If you can come to a program with a comfortable job, you will come to a program with a difficult job too. But if you cannot come to a program when your job is comfortable, you will not come when your job is difficult either. The job was never the real obstacle. It was only ever an excuse.

So let us stop trying to bargain with God. The shadow always moves with the object. [14] If you make your Kṛṣṇa consciousness very strong and healthy, your material needs will follow nicely and become easy. But if you are spiritually sick, then everything in your material life will also be a struggle, a guesswork, and a speculation.

Sincere, heart-centered service is our only business. Leave the results and the timeline to Kṛṣṇa. Do your part, and let Kṛṣṇa do His part.

## **Developing Sincerity Beyond Transactions**

Some of us have a lot of material career aspirations. To achieve them, we think we must invest a massive amount of time and effort, which inevitably pushes our devotional service into the background. But Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī warns us that *prayāsaḥ ca*—endeavoring desperately for material things that are extremely difficult to obtain—instantly destroys bhakti. [15]

Look closely at those who have already achieved the high career positions you are dreaming of. Have you checked how wonderful their lives actually are? When you see them smiling for the camera, do you have any idea of the hollow, screaming vacuum that exists inside their hearts? We think that if we are materially successful, we will finally be happy. That is nothing but a complete lack of faith. Our absolute first priority in life must be Kṛṣṇa consciousness—everything else comes after.

There is a subtler version of this same danger. In an uncertain job market, some devotees consider starting their own business—offering astrology consultations, Vedic services, or similar—as a way to build a more peaceful, bhakti-friendly lifestyle. There is nothing wrong with earning an honest living. But be extremely careful the moment you begin looking at your fellow devotees as prospective customers. The instant a devotee relationship becomes a transaction, sincerity is gone. Take firm shelter of Kṛṣṇa and your spiritual master, and let that shelter carry you steadily through the complexities of society life—rather than turning your spiritual community into your client base.

This feeling of the goal being "too far" comes from a total lack of understanding. You are thinking that *you* are the one who has to cross this gap using your own tiny material strength—as if it is your job to do Kṛṣṇa's part for Him. But Kṛṣṇa only asks us to do our part: *janma karma ca me divyam*—simply try to understand with faith how He appeared and what He did. [16] Whether He grants the result today, tomorrow, or after many lifetimes depends entirely on Him; that is not our department.

When Kṛṣṇa once told Mukunda, *“You will need ten million births to attain Prema,”* [17] Mukunda simply began to dance in surrender. And Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu said, *“Come here, take it.”* Those ten million births' worth of Prema arrived immediately. Why? Because Mukunda did his part, and Kṛṣṇa did His. We are not meant to do our own part while simultaneously trying to jump into Kṛṣṇa's part and calculate the results ourselves. Do your part, and let Kṛṣṇa do His part. Depend on Kṛṣṇa, and the gap will vanish in a single second.

## **The Character and Qualifications of a True Disciple**

How do we protect ourselves when strong lusty desires arise and overpower our minds? Lust never comes “suddenly.” It is already sitting there, hidden inside the crevices of your heart. When it manifests, it simply means your Sādhana is weak and your mind is idle. The secret to conquering lust is to **never be idle!** Be fully, intensely occupied in planning and executing Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Secondly, busily and lovingly care for other devotees. Lust is nothing but the diseased, selfish mirror-image of pure love. To transform it, you must practice active, selfless care for others. When you are busily engaged in making others happy for Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure, lust will find no empty space to enter and will naturally wither away.

This does not mean fighting the mind by shouting at it. Śrīla Prabhupāda's advice was to *neglect* the mind—but neglecting the mind does not mean ignoring it. It means keeping it so fully planned and engaged in service that it has no opening left to disturb you. Look at how Mother Yaśodā planned even her cooking: *“Every day, thieving ghosts steal from the houses of the ladies, because when I let the housemaids prepare the yogurt, they burn the milk. Therefore, today I will not engage the maids—I will do it myself, and I want to do it in such a way that Lord Nārāyaṇa will enjoy it. When Nārāyaṇa enjoys it, He will never steal again.”* [18] That is planning. That is engaging the mind so completely in service to Kṛṣṇa that it simply has no room left to wander.

How do we get to know who our spiritual master is? You pray to Kṛṣṇa. Go to your knees, look at the Deity, and tell Him: *“Please, show me who my spiritual master is.”* He will guide you. But you must be sincere. Plan your alignment tonight. Sleep early, wake up early, do not sit with those who gossip and criticize, and sit strictly with those who chant.

I remember exactly how I met my own spiritual master: in the middle of a heavy kīrtana, completely absorbed in the Holy Name. That absorption is what draws a disciple to a Guru in the first place—not a polished performance, but a heart genuinely lost in the Holy Name.

When can a disciple take the instruction of the spiritual master as their very life and soul? Only when you are surrendered. Without surrender, you can never take the order as your life and soul. If you are not surrendered, your mind will always rise up and challenge: *“Why should I obey you? Why should I do this difficult service?”* When devotees say, *“Oh, I want to follow, but it is just too difficult,”* they are self-deceived. It is not that the instruction is too difficult; it is simply that they have not surrendered yet. The moment you surrender, the strength to execute the order is immediately supplied.

A genuine disciple has **zero duplicity**. They are completely straightforward and transparent in their dealings with everyone, and most of all, with their spiritual master. They have no double life—their private life and their public life are exactly the same.

This same principle of no duplicity extends even to how we prepare Kṛṣṇa's prasādam. The moment a salary enters the picture, the mentality of the cook changes—and that mentality is cooked directly into the food. When we eat food prepared with a commercial, business consciousness, that very consciousness enters us and agitates our minds. This is why paid cooks in our kitchens are so dangerous: it is not bhakti, it is business, even if sixteen rounds are chanted while the cooking is done. I should appreciate every one of you devotees for this reason—because if I take even a single bite of food prepared with the wrong consciousness, my body reacts to it immediately. Whatever we offer Kṛṣṇa, and whatever we feed each other, must be sincere all the way through, with no calculation hidden inside it.

And to protect all of this, morning Sādhana is our ultimate key. Chanting early in the morning is the secret of perfect time management. If you finish your sixteen rounds before 6:30 AM, you will have an incredibly long, peaceful, and powerful day. You will have time to read, time to hear, and time to execute your services with a clear, happy mind. But if you do not chant in the morning, you don’t have a day. Your entire day becomes a frantic, stressful struggle in the mode of passion, where you are running late for everything, screaming at your mind, and achieving nothing of real value.

## **No Obstacle in a Female Body**

Some people say that because bhakti requires intense emotional control and focus, it is very difficult to perform pure devotion in a female body, and that women face too many material obstacles.

This is **completely false!** It is a total misunderstanding.

In reality, women can become pure devotees far faster than men. The only obstacle is when we focus too much on the temporary physical body, forgetting the spiritual treasure we possess within.

Look at our scriptures. In all of our history, in the *Mahābhārata* and the *Rāmāyaṇa*, have you ever read of a single woman who went to the forest to perform austerities and fell down? No! Not a single one! [19]

But how many great male sages, powerful yogīs, and kings went to the forest and fell down? Millions! And who caused their fall down? Women!

In physical combat and spiritual tests, the person who can push you down is always stronger than you. The determination of women is naturally far stronger, more enduring, and more resilient than that of men. All the women who performed austerities in our tradition succeeded without a single exception.

Let us change our minds and stop identifying with these temporary, physical bags of skin. Rise above the bodily platform altogether, tap into that natural, unshakeable determination, and use it to conquer the heart of Kṛṣṇa.

## **The Vow of a Sincere Servant**

Someone once asked me: what is your own plan? How do you intend to serve your Guru and Śrīla Prabhupāda for the rest of your life?

I will tell you plainly. I want to spend my life motivating devotees to rise early in the morning and chant. I want to push devotees to give real attention to their Sādhana. I don't mind criticism—people can say whatever they like about me for it—but I will keep pushing devotees toward Sādhana, because our priority in life is Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Not a diluted version of it. Not “just sādhana”—strong, good Sādhana.

More than anything, I want to help devotees see that we must give up the selfishness that is blocking us. We think we are functioning artificially in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, going through the motions—but Kṛṣṇa consciousness itself is not artificial. It is completely real. It is the selfishness sitting in our own hearts that is blocking us from what is already available to us. We are not purpose-driven, and that is the actual problem.

So let this be completely clear, for me and for you: what is the mood of a devotee who genuinely wants to succeed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness? What should our consciousness be? Not calculation. Not bargaining. Not a double life behind a public face of devotion. Just sincerity—plain, unselfish sincerity.

Because when we understand this and live by it, something happens automatically: **the sincere devotees are protected, and the deviant are exposed.**

## **Chapter 9: Heart Check**

### **Sincerity or Selfishness?**

* Are you dealing with the Deities on the altar with straightforward, loving communication, or has your worship become a silent, dry room where you are not on speaking terms with Lord Kṛṣṇa?
* Look deeply into your prayers from the last year: are you constantly bargaining with Kṛṣṇa for material adjustments—like better hours, a new car, or physical comforts—rather than begging for the costly diamond of pure devotional service?

### **The Temptation of Success**

* When your material and spiritual lives are comfortable and "safe," do you allow bhakti's initial relief to make you lazy and complacent?
* Remember the *Cloud Seven* band: are you resting on the spiritual effort of yesterday, or are you actively trying to expand your Sādhana today?
* Honestly check yourself: am I really serving Kṛṣṇa right now, or just going through the motions? And has any comfort or seniority I've gained quietly turned junior devotees into people I expect to serve me, rather than people I serve?

### **Sincerity in our Daily Actions**

* Are you attempting to perform management during Japa, or stepping onto the altar and into the kitchen before settling your morning rounds?
* Is there any duplicity, hidden motives, or a double life that you are keeping behind your public identity as a devotee?

### **Surrendering to the Instruction**

* When your spiritual master gives you an instruction that feels difficult, does your mind quietly ask, "Why should I obey this?" Be honest: is the instruction actually too difficult, or have you simply not surrendered to it yet?
* Is there a specific order you have been putting off, telling yourself you will get to it "when it's easier"? What would change if you stopped waiting for the strength to come first, and surrendered first instead?
