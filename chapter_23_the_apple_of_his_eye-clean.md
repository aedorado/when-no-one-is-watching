# Chapter 23: The Apple of His Eye

To satisfy Lord Kṛṣṇa is our only business. With our services, with our activities, and in everything we do, our sole motivation must be the desire to please the Lord. That is selflessness. The moment the desire for satisfying ourselves enters, it becomes selfishness, and we can go on performing activities for millions of years without ever being successful in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. 

How do we please Him? Nārada Muni instructs the Pracetās that the Supreme Lord, Janārdana, is very quickly satisfied by a devotee who shows mercy to all living beings, remains satisfied in all conditions, and controls the senses. 

In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (4.31.19), this divine formula is declared:

> *dayayā sarva-bhūteṣu*
> *santuṣṭyā yena kena vā*
> *sarvendriyopaśāntyā ca*
> *tuṣyaty āśu janārdanaḥ*

**Translation by Śrīla Prabhupāda:** 
**“By showing mercy to all living entities, being satisfied somehow or other, and controlling the senses from sense objects, one can very quickly satisfy the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Janārdana.”**

This is not an isolated statement. In the same *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto Four, Chapter Eleven—very likely in the exchange where Svāyambhuva Manu instructs his grandson Dhruva Mahārāja—almost the same thing is said: by tolerance, mercy, friendship, and equal vision toward all living beings, the Lord is quickly satisfied. Two verses, in two chapters of the same Canto, teaching us the very same formula.

---

## **The Piece of Apple**

What is mercy? What is compassion? 

To have the interest to show mercy to all living entities means we must be compassionate. Compassion means that you cannot tolerate the sufferings of others. A selfish person can never be compassionate; only a selfless person can have real compassion.

If we look at our own history, we must ask: how did our supreme preceptor, Śrīla Prabhupāda, preach? He did not convince us through dry, academic philosophy. He did not conquer our hearts by showing off his intellectual scholarship. He convinced us by his personal, sweet behavior. It was his mercy. It was his compassion. 

In his early days of preaching, a typical lecture by Śrīla Prabhupāda was very brief—only twenty minutes! But at the end of the lecture, he would personally share a small piece of apple with everyone in the room [1]. 

That simple, sweet gesture—that piece of apple—is what made people surrender and become devotees. It was his compassion, not the philosophical complexity, that entered their hearts. The Lord is pleased when we greet others with that same spirit of mercy, friendship, and tolerance.

---

## **Character is Power**

To show mercy to all living entities means we must preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness all over the world so that people can make their lives successful. And "all living entities" is not a figure of speech—the word *sarva-bhūteṣu* covers every one of the 8,400,000 species of life, not only human beings. Even the trees, the animals, and the insects benefit when the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra is chanted loudly; that transcendental vibration reaches them too. But we must understand what true preaching actually is. 

Preaching is not an exercise or a drama. It is not a stage show where you go to some theater, practice acting, and then come to the stage to make a demonstration. Preaching is the manifestation of Vaiṣṇava character. 

**“A preacher is one who has character. It is character that preaches, not philosophy. Knowledge does not preach; knowledge cannot transform somebody, but character can transform. Character preaches—that is a preacher. Therefore, knowledge is not power—character is power.”**

We cannot please Kṛṣṇa by fame, beauty, strength, or knowledge—whatever we prize in this material world. We can please Him only by how much we help other devotees in their Kṛṣṇa consciousness. And to help, you must be ready to suffer. If you are not ready to suffer, you cannot help anybody—not even yourself.

---

## **The Test of Tolerance**

Tolerance was named right alongside mercy in that formula from the Fourth Canto, and it is tested hardest not with our friends but with our enemies. How do we respond to those who do not think well of us? How do we treat someone who speaks badly about us, or who even plans to harm us? Do we look at them with tolerance? Are we merciful toward them?

Are we treating even the Yakṣas with tolerance [7]? That is the real test of the mercy this formula is asking for—whether we can let go of vengeance, not just extend goodwill where it costs us nothing.

We talk endlessly about mercy, yet we struggle with basic, gentle Vaiṣṇava dealings. Hare Kṛṣṇa devotees do not even greet each other! 

Look at the tiny ants. Have you ever seen an ant pass another ant on a wall without greeting? I am talking about ants, not human beings! When two ants meet on a wall, they stop and kiss each other before they pass. They check on each other: *“How are you doing?”* But a Hare Kṛṣṇa devotee will often only greet you if they want something from you! If they don't need anything, they will walk right past you as if you are a stone!

Why are we suffering in this movement? Sincere devotees sometimes ask me why we face so much suffering. I tell them straight: we suffer because we are duplicitous. We are still deeply attached to our dirty, sinful habits, and we are not feeling bad about how filthy our hearts actually are. We want to speak big philosophy to others, but we fail to apply tolerance, mercy, and friendship in our own daily interactions. 

Śrīla Prabhupāda notes that it is the duty of an advanced devotee on the *madhyama-adhikārī* platform—the second stage of devotional perfection—to act in accordance with this verse [2]. A *kaniṣṭha-adhikārī* does not have the Vaiṣṇava character to affect others because their hearts are still empty of purity. A *madhyama-adhikārī* bhakta has character, and because they are free of duplicity, their only interest is to help and liberate all conditioned souls.

Showing this compassion does not mean merely taking care of people's bodies. The world is already full of charitable foundations doing that—giving aid, education, hospitals. But without purifying the heart, people cannot give up their bad character, and so the suffering continues. The heart has to be transformed, and that transformation can only come through the mercy of a pure devotee who has the knowledge and the qualification to give it.

---

## **Relieving His Anxiety**

If purity is the force that transforms hearts, how does a disciple please the spiritual master? 

Śrīla Prabhupāda once explained: **“The duty of a disciple is to relieve the spiritual master of his anxiety. A disciple should never be a liability or a drain on the Guru. But these days, we are liabilities. We are not disciples. The bona fide disciple's absolute duty is to relieve the spiritual master of his anxiety [3].”**

How do we relieve his anxiety? We do it by supporting his preaching, facilitating his mission, and taking active risks to expand his service. 

Pure devotees make incredible, life-threatening sacrifices to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Traveling from country to country is not a comfortable vacation; dealing with embassies, visas, and physical hardships is full of intense anxiety. Yet they do it because their hearts bleed out of compassion for the conditioned souls who are suffering all over the world. 

We love hearing about pure devotees who risked their lives like this—but we do not want to be like them. If we don't want to be like them, how can we ever go where they have gone? They came here for exactly one reason: to teach us the process. So we should stop deceiving ourselves into thinking that we can reach the same place by some other way. There is no other way. There is only one way.

Understanding what the spiritual master wants and executing it is what makes a true disciple. Śrīla Prabhupāda noted that all the great previous spiritual leaders had established their centers in the Holy Dham of Māyāpur, and he expressed his own deep desire to have a special center there as well [4].

Let us look at a beautiful, sacred pastime of Lord Caitanya and Haridāsa Ṭhākura that shows the ultimate standard of this anxiety [5]. 

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu would go to Haridāsa Ṭhākura and tell him: *“Haridāsa, I have anxiety.”* Haridāsa Ṭhākura would ask what anxiety the Lord could possibly have, since His desires are automatically fulfilled. Mahāprabhu explained: *“I came to deliver all living entities. I have done that for the humans, through this congregational chanting. But what about those who cannot chant—the plants, the trees, the animals? How will I be able to deliver them?”*

Haridāsa Ṭhākura answered: *“My Lord, You have already done it. When You chanted, it echoed. That echo was their chanting. You have already delivered them.”*

The Lord and His pure representative carry this burning anxiety for the suffering souls, and our only duty as disciples is to step forward and help relieve that anxiety.

It does not matter to a pure devotee even if he has to go to hell to preach. The Supreme Lord lives in the heart of a hog just as much as He lives in Vaikuṇṭha, and a pure devotee remains a pure devotee anywhere, because his constant remembrance of the Lord never breaks. In truth, a pure devotee does not really see heaven or hell at all—wherever he looks, all he sees is Goloka. And wherever he goes, he turns that place into Vaikuṇṭha; even hell becomes Vaikuṇṭha when a pure devotee enters it. That is why Nārada Muni himself went to the hellish planets—not because he had any business there, but simply to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

There is a companion pastime that shows the same selfless compassion from the other side. Janaka Mahārāja, on his way back to Godhead, asked to see the hellish planets out of compassion, to see what was happening there. Standing at the door, the breeze from his own body began to relieve the suffering inside, and the souls there begged him to wait. So he waited—and forgot, for a time, that he was on his way back to Godhead, because his presence was helping them. He had no business with the suffering, but he stayed anyway, simply to show mercy, until he was finally sent on his way. That is what a selfless devotee looks like: so absorbed in showing mercy that even liberation can wait.

---

## **The Divine Formula: Forget the Problem**

Many devotees are constantly overwhelmed by personal problems, health issues, bad karma, and material anxieties. They spend their days meditating on their sickness, or complaining about what they lack. 

**“If you continue to think of your problem, you will get problems continuously, because that is what you are meditating upon! When the problem becomes your deity, it will never go away, because you are always calling the problem to come. Forget about the problem, and the problem will forget you! This is a divine formula.”**

Stop meditating on your bad health, your financial struggles, or your material anxieties. If you keep meditating on your bad health, you will remain in bad health! Instead, throw your mind and your intelligence into planning how to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness and how to help others become free of their anxieties. 

To preach, we must first be satisfied ourselves in our own Kṛṣṇa consciousness. We cannot distribute frustration to others; we must distribute the process of becoming free from anxiety. If you are already full of anxiety, how can you preach? What will you give to the people? Frustration! 

What is in your mind will be transferred to the people you are preaching to. They will hear it directly in your voice: *“You are trying to teach me something you don't even believe yourself. I can hear the doubt and frustration in your voice. Why do you want me to be like you?”* People want to be with someone who is spiritually successful, happy, and satisfied.

When your mind is fully engaged in planning how to make Kṛṣṇa consciousness easy for others, you are instantly transported to Goloka. Your mind is absorbed in His service, and Kṛṣṇa Himself smiles with you.

How do you know your service is pleasing to Him? Nobody has to tell you that your belly is full when you are eating—you simply know it yourself. In the same way, when you are advancing in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, you do not need anyone to inform you: because Kṛṣṇa is pleased, you are pleased. That joy floods directly into your own heart, and you know your service has been accepted.

The verse names one more piece of this formula: controlling the senses. But the senses are not controlled by denying them—that is artificial, and it does not work, because Kṛṣṇa consciousness is not the opposite of material life. It is not the opposite of anything. It is the actual reality; it is life itself. What we experience as the material world is only a reflection of the spiritual. So our duty is to control the senses, not to deny them, by engaging them fully in Kṛṣṇa's service rather than starving them. And when Kṛṣṇa sees us trying to climb out of the trap of material existence in this way, He becomes very happy, and He Himself supplies everything we need.

---

## **Expanding the Director's Business**

When you forget your own selfish anxieties and spend your life planning how to reclaim lost souls, you become **“the apple of Kṛṣṇa's eye.”** 

Kṛṣṇa can never forget a devotee who is deeply concerned with supporting His mission. In fact, He declares that the highest opulence of His divine character is His eagerness to reclaim the conditioned souls. 

**“When you support that mission, Kṛṣṇa becomes your personal supporter. Why? Because you are doing His duty! It is completely natural. If you are working very hard in an office, your director will like you and promote you, because he sees you are expanding his business. If you are expanding Kṛṣṇa’s business, your promotion back to Godhead is guaranteed.”**

We must become selfless. Sincere devotees are always selfless. When you give Kṛṣṇa to people, you get Kṛṣṇa back!

But what we so often show instead is hard-heartedness. Failing to greet a devotee is not a small rudeness—it is an offense. And one cannot commit that offense and still expect to make progress in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. It does not work that way.

---

## **The Art of Association**

How do we maintain this standard of satisfaction when surrounded by the whimsical, disturbing behaviors of others in community life? 

We should not allow the misbehavior of others to become our meditation. If someone behaves foolishly, and we have the opportunity to advise them, we should do so affectionately. If we cannot, we look for someone close to them to address it. But if nothing works, we must immediately remove our mind and continue our service. 

When someone fails to greet us, or treats us coldly, that is their nature, not our problem—unless we make it our problem by letting it disturb our mind. Our own duty is simply to keep greeting them. If we truly care for the person, we can ask them gently what is wrong, and let them tell us; then we can clear it up. But we should not expect the difficulties of this material world to disappear. If they disappeared, this would not be the material world—it would be Vaikuṇṭha.

A devotee once asked how we can transform the whole world. Do not waste your time on that, came the answer—finish your own business. Only when everyone develops that same desire to liberate every conditioned soul will such a thing even be possible, and our attachment to sense gratification is so deeply our nature that it will not change overnight. Our duty is simply to work our own way out.

With those whose behavior is not up to standard, we should keep some distance—not because we hate them, but because closeness is not helping either of us. Distance is not hatred; it protects both sides. We come together only for service, and once the service is finished, we let them be, with their own struggle, rather than staying entangled in it.

**“We should learn how to take all the good things from people and leave the bad things with them. That is the practice.”**

Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī categorized the different grades of devotees so we would know exactly how to relate with everyone without committing offenses [6]. If we are strong in our early morning Sādhana, the material disturbances of others will simply bounce off our chests. But if we are weak, we will be affected by everything. 

Let us build Vaiṣṇava character, relieve our spiritual master's anxieties, expand Kṛṣṇa's business, and become the apple of His eye.

## **Chapter 23: Heart Check**

### **Character over Scholarship**

* Do you focus entirely on memorizing philosophy and giving high-sounding lectures, while failing to show tolerance, mercy, and friendship in your daily dealings with Vaiṣṇavas?
* *The Ant Test:* Am I greeting my godbrothers and godsisters with genuine warmth, or do I only interact with others when I want to get something from them?

### **An Asset vs. A Liability**

* Examine your relationship with your spiritual master or authority: Are you a liability who constantly dumps personal anxieties on him, or are you an asset who actively works to relieve his anxiety?
* What is one concrete, practical action you can perform this week to make his preaching easier—handling a logistical burden, supporting a project he wants built, or simply removing one thing he would otherwise have to worry about himself?

### **The Formula of Selflessness**

* Are you constantly meditating on your bad karma, health issues, and material problems, making them the "deity" of your mind?
* How will you apply the divine formula this week to forget your own problems by throwing your mind into planning how to reclaim other conditioned souls?
