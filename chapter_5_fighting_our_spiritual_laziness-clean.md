# Chapter 5: Fighting Our Spiritual Laziness

What is the greatest lack in our modern spiritual movement? 

If we look around honestly, what is missing is a deep, unshakeable dedication to the spiritual master. Today, there is a very prominent disease of disconnection. Many of us are physically present in the temple, but we are disconnected from the heart of our Guru. 

Let us look at a person who had a truly deep connection: my own spiritual master, Śrīla Gurudeva, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami. I was fortunate to serve him for many years, and I saw his qualities firsthand. He did not live an artificial, superficial spiritual life.

Even people entirely outside our movement could see this in him—even an American author who had written many books on leadership traveled all the way to Gītā Nagarī just to confirm with his own eyes that Gurudeva actually lived what he wrote.

He communicated with his Guru, Śrīla Prabhupāda, every day through constant, submissive prayer and accountability. Every night, before he went to sleep, he would look at his diary and quietly report his day to Śrīla Prabhupāda, asking his Guru whether he had left anything undone. That daily review was his constant, living communication and accountability with his spiritual master.

This same uncompromised connection guided Śrīla Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami during challenging GBC leadership meetings. When a highly sensitive or political issue was being debated, he would not simply jump into the dispute or depend on his own intellect. Instead, he would step aside, even go to the toilet at times, and pray submissively to Śrīla Prabhupāda, listening for guidance and asking how his master would address the issue if he were physically present. He would wait for the spiritual inspiration, and only then return to the room to present what was pleasing to his Guru.

During one particular committee debate about whether to bring in Śrīla Prabhupāda's godbrothers to lead ISKCON, Gurudev left the meeting, went to the toilet to pray, and Śrīla Prabhupāda told him no. He returned to the room and requested on his Guru's behalf that everyone take a break to pray and seek inspiration from Prabhupāda. When other leaders opposed him and wanted to push through the agenda, he stood his ground on spiritual principles. He calmly told them that they were free to do their own thing, but since he had prayed and received a negative instruction from Śrīla Prabhupāda, he would not compromise. He made it clear that his stand was not based on his own false ego, declaring that we are all disciples and all have the right to communicate with our Guru. 

That quality of uncompromised, prayerful loyalty is extremely rare today. 

---

### **The Disease of Laziness**

Śrīla Gurudeva used his massive intelligence to design a Kṛṣṇa consciousness that is sustainable—both materially and spiritually. When I started, he gave us a single, heavy word that has stayed with me to this day: **“lazy”**. He used that single word, but there are so many deep things inside it. He warned us that we are spiritually lazy. 

Because of our laziness, we cannot access the incredible wealth we have already been given. In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, we have everything already. Within Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books, he has packed the realizations of all the greatest ācāryas—Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī, and Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī. He put all these great sādhus into his purports for us.

But because we are lazy, we don’t study Prabhupāda’s books properly. We read them superficially, and when we see nothing, we think we have exhausted the teachings. Then we start jumping here and there, “sādhu-hopping” to other movements and other speakers, looking for some fresh excitement. 

I must ask you: if your belly is completely full, will you go around looking for food? No! If your belly is full, you don't look for food anywhere. So if we are still searching outside, it is because we are spiritually empty. We have not actually digested the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. 

And when we do that, we look like a joke, and Gurudeva chides us: **“Lazy, lazy. Lazy. Lazy.”**

We say, *“Oh, I have read all of that, but there is nothing there... so I want another sādhu.”* Is that what we are doing? **“Lazy! Lazy, that is the problem. Laziness.”** 

Śrīla Gurudeva did not train us to be spiritual nomads. He trained us to be loyal—unshakably loyal—to the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and to Śrīla Prabhupāda.

---

### **Jñāna vs. Bhakti**

There is a dangerous direction many of us are taking. I see it every day. We are moving toward *jñāna*—dry, intellectual knowledge—instead of *bhakti*. 

Now, let us try to understand this clearly. Knowledge itself is not bad. Knowledge of the scriptures is an important part of our practice. But the little we read from the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is often used only as a knowledge aspect, not as *bhakti*. Why do we love *jñāna*? Because it only entertains our intelligence.

But does dry knowledge change our character? No. 

When we operate only on the platform of dry knowledge, our daily dealings with each other become unpalatable. We speak big philosophy, but we are miserable, disturbed at every step. That is the result of dry knowledge. It is unpalatable.

*Bhakti* is completely different. Bhakti does not care to entertain your intellectual ego. Bhakti goes straight to the heart and destroys all the nauseous things, the dirt, and the duplicity inside.[1]

And when that dirt is removed, what is left? Sweet, pure, and loving dealings with each other.[2] Anyone who comes into contact with a devotee like that is immediately affected by their purity. 

My own spiritual master, Śrīla Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, lived this sweet standard of *bhakti*. He was deeply personal and caring in his dealings, providing physical, mental, mystical, and spiritual protection to his followers. One day, while he was giving class, his godsister quietly came to the door because she needed to ask her husband something urgent in the kitchen—but she would not interrupt. Gurudeva noticed her standing there, paused his class, and said, "Meet your wife, she needs something." Only once her husband had gone to help her did he continue speaking. Even in the middle of teaching, nothing escaped his attention when a devotee needed him. That same nature stayed with him to his very last breath, as I will describe later in this book. That is the sweet fruit of *bhakti*.

---

### **The Transparent Postman**

How do we protect ourselves from falling into the trap of false prestige and pride? 

We must always remember: **“I am representing my spiritual master. I am representing his service.”** He has engaged us in this service for our own protection—not because we have become someone important.

But we forget that, and block our own way. We start thinking, *“I am the one.”* The moment we do, we begin trying to carry the Guru’s load on our own head, and we do not have the strength to carry that load. Difficulty after difficulty starts pressing in on us.

Śrīla Gurudeva taught us a beautiful analogy for this: we are simply **postmen**. A postman is given a valuable, sealed parcel to deliver to a specific address. The parcel does not belong to the postman. His only duty is to carry it carefully and deliver it exactly as it is, without opening it, altering it, or claiming it as his own property. 

But what do we do? The moment we are given some service, some position or authority, we immediately want to open the parcel and put ourselves in the center. This is criminal.

Let go of the need to be the boss. Keep the parcel closed. Deliver it exactly as Śrīla Prabhupāda gave it to us, and you will get a promotion. That promotion is spiritual strength and absolute protection.

---

### **How to Pray on the Battlefield**

Let us pause here. We must understand how to actually pray on the battlefield of spiritual life. Śrīla Gurudeva taught us the importance of prayer—it is one of the nine limbs of bhakti Prahlāda Mahārāja describes to his father,[3] and we should do it every day. Sincere prayers should come from the heart, not just the lips. 

Let us look at two stunning prayers that we should carry in our hearts like spiritual armor.

#### **1. The Prayer of the “Foolish Beggar”**
This is a prayer written by Śrīla Gurudeva himself. He taught us to beg Kṛṣṇa every day:
> *“My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, I am a beggar. I do not know how to beg. My fortune is that I have someone who begs for me.”*[4]

When we are too fallen, too distracted, or too spiritually bankrupt to even know what to ask Kṛṣṇa for, this prayer is our lifeline. We are simply depending on the begging power of our Guru.

#### **2. The Armor of a Representative**
Before you go out to preach, manage, or perform any responsible service, you are stepping onto a battlefield. If you do not pray first, you will end up serving your own ego. Here is the prayer we should make:
> *“Gurudev, I am representing you. Let me not become the master. Let me not become the master. I better remain like a servant. So you protect me. Always protect me so I remain a servant.”*

This prayer is not a cheap formality; it is our active shield. It removes the load of “doership” from our head and places our service under the absolute protection of the Guru.

---

### **Our Weapon on the Battlefield**

We must wake up to the reality of where we are. We are not in a playground. We are on a **battlefield**. 

* **The Battlefield:** Your own consciousness.
* **The Target:** Māyā.
* **Our Weapon:** Hearing.

This is not casual listening. This is *śravaṇam*—the very first of the nine processes of devotional service[3]—hearing about Kṛṣṇa from scripture and from sādhus, with full attention and faith.

Every day, we are going out to fight. And if you step onto this battlefield without your weapon, you are not going to fight—you are going to get killed! 

So every day we should pray: **“I am going out to fight. I do not know what you have arranged out there, but as you have arranged it, please also arrange for my safety, because I am representing you.”**

Chanting our rounds and hearing *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is not a fashion, and it is not an optional hobby. It is our ammunition. When we hear attentively, we are reloading our weapon. 

If you do not hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, your mind will automatically start hearing about Māyā. 

So let us stop being lazy. Let us stop offering Kṛṣṇa our dry, academic *jñāna*. Let us pray with intense, serious sincerity, and become simple, transparent postmen.

---

### **Uncompromised Loyalty: The Yugoslavian Trial**

My beloved spiritual father, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, showed us what it means to be unshakeably loyal under the most extreme, life-threatening trials. 

During the height of his book distribution in Eastern Europe under the communist regime, anyone distributing religious materials was treated as a dangerous criminal. He was arrested and locked in a cell in Yugoslavia. 

A female officer opened the cell door, walked inside, and looked at him. She said: *“Do you know the offense you have committed? You are going to jail... However, I have the power to release you right now on one condition: you must enjoy with me.”* 

Think about the sheer weight of that test! He was alone, in a foreign land, facing a brutal prison sentence, and Māyā offered him a cheap, immediate escape through sense gratification. But Gurudev did not hesitate for a fraction of a second. He looked her in the eye and said: 

*“I have many people with me here. My spiritual master is seeing me, and he is seeing you. You think none of my members are here, but my Guru is here, and he is watching everything.”* 

As soon as he said that, the officer simply said, "Please go," and released him. That is the power of uncompromised disciplic connection. Māyā cannot stand before a disciple who refuses to play double-games.

---

### **Our Protective Boundaries**

This same protective power was there in his personal dealings with me. I remember when I was young, I wanted to pursue a music project that I thought would bring incredible fame and money to our preaching. In my young mind, I thought this was the ultimate service. 

But Gurudev looked at me with deep concern. He did not care about the money or the superficial fame. He cared about my soul. He looked me in the eye and said: 

*“Stop the music! Stop! If you do music, you will get married, and you will not be in the movement.”*

If I had gone ahead, it would have brought so much external success, but my spiritual foundation would have been completely ruined. He was ready to stop me, to block my path, to keep me protected within the safe, humble boundaries of disciplic service. He wanted things to be real, not an artificial show.

I have said this many times: it was not because he was Black like many of us that we accepted him as our spiritual master. It was the qualities he had—qualities exactly like this one—that attracted us to him.

Let us drop our spiritual laziness. Let us stop looking for external excitement or dry intellectual validation. Let us surrender our hearts to following the instructions of our Guru, and experience the sweet, uncompromised safety of disciplic protection.

## **Chapter 5: Heart Check**

### **Am I Wearing the Spiritual Armor?**
Before stepping out into the world for your daily work, your family responsibilities, or your preaching service:

* Do you pray to represent your spiritual master submissively, or do you rush out unprotected, trying to carry the heavy load of doership on your own head?
* Do you chant your rounds with the humble awareness of a beggar, depending on the Guru's prayers, or has your Japa become a dry, mechanical ritual?

### **My Nightly Account**
Like Śrīla Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, who examined his diary every night to ask if his service was done exactly as desired:

* Before you go to sleep tonight, put away all devices, look into your heart, and ask yourself:
 1. *My dear Guru, did I serve your instructions with my full heart today, or did I let laziness and ego take over?*
 2. *In my dealings with other devotees today, did I speak with sweet, pure Bhakti, or was I critical, political, and unpalatable?*
