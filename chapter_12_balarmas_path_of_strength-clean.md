# Chapter 12: Balarāma’s Path of Strength

If you look at Him carefully, Balarāma is always mentioned as the senior brother of Kṛṣṇa. But is He really the senior brother? No, He is actually the junior brother! Who came first? His junior, Kṛṣṇa. The senior sent the junior to go and check, to supervise, before the senior Himself arrives. Balarāma came after expanding from Kṛṣṇa. They are two bodies, but they are one soul, one blood—except one is white and one is black. 

Everything began from Kṛṣṇa, who divided Himself into two: Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma. And from this original division, Balarāma’s only meditation, His intense, burning desire, is to serve Kṛṣṇa.

Lord Balarāma is called the safest form of Kṛṣṇa. In the Western traditions, they speak of the Trinity: God the Father, God the Son, and the Holy Spirit. In our eternal theology, Balarāma represents that supreme, protecting will. And because He is the safest form, His absolute, undivided duty is to serve. 

---

## **His Intense Desire to Serve**

In His intense desire to serve, Lord Balarāma expands Himself into everything we see in both the material and spiritual worlds. 

First, He expands into the *Catur-vyūha*—Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha. Then He expands into Mahā-Viṣṇu, Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, and Ananta Śeṣa [1]. Even Lord Nṛsiṁha, Varāha, Matsya, Kūrma, and Rāmacandra—all of the great incarnations—are also Balarāma's expansions [13]. All of these are Balarāma! Every single one of them is manifested strictly for the purpose of service. Only *one* form of Balarāma is used for dancing and playing with Kṛṣṇa. All other forms are used for creation, maintenance, and the physical heavy-lifting of the universes. 

All the sciences we study—algebra, mathematics, physics, or chemistry—are not dry, secular subjects. They are simply the engineering of Lord Balarāma. Even a weapon like the Brahmāstra is sensible, coming from Balarāma's own intelligence; but when the same energy is corrupted and cut off from Him, it becomes the atomic bomb—senseless, contaminated, and useless, good only for destruction. Even the planetary systems, the innumerable universes, the oceans, the mountains, the rivers—everything is maintained and taken care of by Lord Balarāma. 

Even Kṛṣṇa’s personal, intimate objects are expansions of Balarāma! Kṛṣṇa’s dhotī is Balarāma, His chador is Balarāma, His shoes, His bed, His chair, His flute, and His crown—all are Balarāma’s body, transformed into objects of comfort strictly to serve His brother. In the spiritual world, Kṛṣṇa’s Sandhinī and Saṁvit potencies are placed completely under Balarāma’s charge to maintain the entire structural reality of the spiritual and material realms [2]. All these manifestations exist strictly for service.

---

## **Balarāma Holds the Key to Kṛṣṇa**

Because Balarāma maintains everything, He holds the key to understanding Kṛṣṇa. Balarāma holds the key to understanding Kṛṣṇa.

The less intelligent class of people, without consulting the authorities, speculate and tell lies. If you open an English dictionary, what does it say? It says Kṛṣṇa is just an “avatar of Viṣṇu”. It says *Bhakti* is a mere “sentiment”. They will never have access to the reality until they take shelter of Balarāma. 

I remember visiting a devotee’s house where one of the guests was a highly qualified scientist. He came to me and said, *“Maharaj, we have got to the point where we are all completely confused.”* 

I looked at him and said, *“Yes, Prabhu, and you will remain confused! You will never get out of your confusion until you take shelter of Balarāma. Only when you take shelter of Balarāma will your eyes open, because He holds the key to the locks of your intellect.”* Balarāma holds the key to understanding Kṛṣṇa.

---

## **Garbhodāna and Saṅkarṣaṇa**

Before we walk into a beautifully constructed temple room, an engineer must first build the structure. The director of the temple did not stand there in the mud laying bricks; the director only walked in *after* the construction was complete. In the exact same way, the universal construction was done under Balarāma’s direction. And only when the scene was fully set did Kṛṣṇa enter. 

But when they were coming, Balarāma had to prepare the way. To teach us how compulsory and sacred the *Garbhādāna-saṁskāra* is for all human beings, Lord Balarāma Himself performed it. If we do not perform this saṁskāra, Śrīla Prabhupāda warns us that the child we produce will not truly be a human being, but will remain on the animal platform [14]. Balarāma was the very first person in the universe to perform this sacred act of preparing the womb. 

He entered the womb of Devakī first, installing His expansion, Ananta Śeṣa, to make Devakī's womb a comfortable place for Kṛṣṇa. Once Balarāma had made sure everything was complete and secure, Kṛṣṇa ordered Yogamāyā to transfer Balarāma from Devakī’s womb to Rohiṇī’s womb in Gokula [3]. 

When Kṛṣṇa gave this instruction, Yogamāyā was hesitant and worried, feeling that she did not have the power to drag the supreme brother of the Lord. But Kṛṣṇa insisted that this was her assigned service, so Yogamāyā executed the order and transferred Him. 

To the non-devotees and the demons, this transfer appeared as a tragic miscarriage, and Kaṁsa’s guards reported that Devakī's seventh child was dead. But to the devotees, it was the ultimate divine trick to protect them. Because Balarāma was dragged from Devakī’s womb to Rohiṇī’s womb, He was given the name **Saṅkarṣaṇa**—which literally means “He who was dragged”.

---

## **The Dragging of the Yamunā**

Because He was dragged, He sometimes behaves exactly like that! Everything has an effect. 

Later in His pastimes, Lord Balarāma was enjoying on the banks of the Yamunā River, having taken some Vāruṇī liquor. He called out to the Yamunā River, asking her to come closer so He could play in her waters with His friends. 

But the Yamunā River refused to move, thinking that He was acting like a drunkard and was contaminated. 

Balarāma’s eyes flared with anger. He shouted, *“You called me a drunkard? You think I am contaminated? Okay! Let me teach you exactly how a drunkard behaves!”* 

He snatched His mighty plow, dug it deep into her banks, and dragged the Yamunā violently toward Him! The Yamunā River was terrified, fell at His feet, and begged for His forgiveness. Even though Balarāma forgave her, the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* records that she was permanently divided, flowing in crooked curves to this day because she was dragged by Balarāma’s plow [4].

---

## **Akrūra and His Heavy Duty**

For eleven years, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma lived in Vṛndāvana, tending the cows and killing every single demon Kaṁsa sent. Balarāma was always the highly protective elder brother. When Mother Yaśodā, out of intense parental anxiety, wanted to go to the forest with them, Balarāma stepped forward and spoke the truth to protect Kṛṣṇa’s fun, telling her that they only go there to play and that if she came, she would spoil their games. 

But the peaceful days in Vṛndāvana were interrupted. Every demon Kaṁsa sent to Vṛndāvana had been killed, one after another, without exception. Finally, one of his own men pointed out the obvious: *"You keep sending demons to fight them in their own territory, and none of them return. Why not invite them here instead, to your own house, where you have the advantage?"* Kaṁsa's response was pure, bitter sarcasm: *"Thank you very much for this excellent advice—now, after I have already lost every single one of my friends. But very well, thank you anyway."* So Kaṁsa sent Akrūra to Vṛndāvana to invite Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā for a wrestling match. 

Why Akrūra? Akrūra was a pure devotee, but he had also once participated in a conspiracy against the residents of Vṛndāvana, and for that offense he carried a curse. Yet Kṛṣṇa does not use a conditioned soul to represent Him in His own pastimes—just as no company hires someone with one year of experience for a role that needs ten. Only a qualified, pure devotee could be trusted to bring Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā without simply becoming another dead demon. Akrūra knew Kaṁsa’s wicked plan, but he also knew that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma are the Supreme Lords. 

When Akrūra arrived in Vṛndāvana, he did something shocking: he heavily chastised Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma! He looked at the two brothers and asked: *“Kṛṣṇa! Balarāma! What are you doing? How can you sit here so peacefully taking care of cows and enjoying yourselves in the forest, while your own mother and father are being tortured every day in Kaṁsa’s prison [5]? Every day, Kaṁsa enters their cell, draws his sharp sword, and threatens to cut off their heads! Have you forgotten your duty? The demigods begged you to appear to kill Kaṁsa, and yet you are sitting here quietly!”* 

This brings us to a deep question: was Akrūra actually committing an offense, or was he serving Kṛṣṇa’s arrangement? 

The truth is, Akrūra was executing a heavy duty. He had to say these words to break the sweet, tranquil absorption of Vṛndāvana so that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma would fulfill Their ultimate mission. Hearing this heavy chastisement, They climbed onto Akrūra’s chariot.

---

## **The Footprint in the Dust**

When the Gopīs saw the chariot preparing to leave, their hearts broke. They threw themselves on the ground in front of the horses, weeping and screaming. The Gopīs’ love was so intense that Kṛṣṇa’s heart melted. He jumped down from the chariot, and the Gopīs immediately took Him and ran deep into the forest. 

Balarāma, sitting on the chariot, did not even see Kṛṣṇa jump down! When Akrūra asked where Kṛṣṇa had gone, Balarāma climbed down and looked at the dust of Vṛndāvana. 

He saw Kṛṣṇa’s footprint in the dust and began to follow it. He followed it deep into the thick bushes until He found Kṛṣṇa sitting in a clearing, completely surrounded by the weeping Gopīs. 

Balarāma stepped into the clearing, looked at His younger brother, and said: *“My dear brother, please, come. Let us go. Akrūra is in intense anxiety. A devotee must never cause anxiety to another devotee. What is immediate and compulsory right now is our duty to Mathurā. Come.”* 

This is a vital lesson: **a devotee must never cause anxiety to another devotee.** Because Balarāma—their beloved *Dauji Baba*—was the one leading Him, the Gopīs did not say a single word. Out of deep respect for Balarāma’s authority, they let them go.

---

## **The Mystery of Vṛndāvana vs. Mathurā**

Akrūra drove the chariot as fast as he could. They reached the border of Vṛndāvana at Akrūra Ghāṭ. 

This border is the ultimate, mysterious boundary of our philosophy. *Vrajendranandana* Kṛṣṇa—the original, sweet cowherd boy of Vṛndāvana—never leaves Vṛndāvana [6]. He did not take a single step outside the boundary. At the very border, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma stepped off and returned secretly into the forests of Braj. Simultaneously, Their formal, royal expansions—Vāsudeva and Saṅkarṣaṇa (the *Yadu Kumāra* expansions)—manifested and went to Mathurā on the chariot. 

When Akrūra went to take his ritual dip in the river, he saw Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma on the chariot, and simultaneously saw Lord Viṣṇu resting majestically on Ananta Śeṣa in the water. We do not need to over-explain this mystery. Kṛṣṇa Himself tells us how to approach it: *"Only by undivided devotional service can I be understood as I am, standing before you, and can thus be seen directly"* (*Bhagavad-gītā* 11.54) [9]. Kṛṣṇa wants us to enter His mysteries through undivided, submissive devotional service, not through dry intellectual speculation.

---

## **Pain That Brings Pleasure**

After Kaṁsa was killed, the royal brothers eventually moved to Dvārakā, leaving Vṛndāvana in the excruciating fire of separation (*vipralambha*). 

Why did Kṛṣṇa arrange this? Feeling separation is what keeps the love eternally increasing. Kṛṣṇa Himself said it plainly: *“Sambhoga is sweet, but vipralambha is sweeter. Therefore, I purposely created this for My devotees, so we can enter into it and really experience it.”* [12] Union is sweet, but separation is sweeter still.

Gurudeva gives us a simple, beautiful explanation of this spiritual separation: **“Spiritual pain brings pleasure because it is not pain. It is actually pleasure.”** In this material world, pain is dry and exhausting. But in the spiritual realm, the tears of separation are sweet and full of ecstatic bliss, making our love grow to infinite heights.

The great saintly Śrīla Gaura Govinda Mahārāja once gave a beautiful, practical experiment to help us understand this [15]. Imagine you take raw sugarcane juice and place it on a fire, boiling it and boiling it. Before it solidifies into dry jaggery, while it is still in a thick, semi-liquid state—how does it taste? Incredibly sweet!

Now, if you want to taste the sweetest part of it, you must drink it while it is still boiling hot. If you drink it hot, it will burn your lips and your tongue. But because the taste is so staggeringly sweet, you will refuse to stop drinking! You will feel the burn, yet you will take another gulp, and you will never spit it out. And the miracle of this spiritual sugarcane juice is that you will never end up in a hospital—your lips will burn, but your soul will be cured of the material disease forever.

This is what we call *prema*—pure, ecstatic love of Godhead. Under its influence, the forest becomes home, and home becomes forest. Night becomes day, and day becomes night. Suffering becomes happiness, and happiness becomes suffering. Since we must experience pain in this world one way or another, let us choose the one that is best. Lord Balarāma came to give us this very nectar, and He is distributing it eternally—the remnant remains available forever, because it never stops.

---

## **Balarāma Returns Alone to Console Vṛndāvana**

After some time, Lord Balarāma could no longer tolerate His family’s pain in Vṛndāvana. Having taken Lord Kṛṣṇa’s permission, He mounted His chariot and traveled alone to Nanda Gokula. Śrī Rāghava Gosvāmī points out that this same journey is also recorded in the *Hari-vaṁśa* [10].

Śukadeva Gosvāmī calls Him **Balabhadra Kuru Śreṣṭha** [7]. Here, Gurudeva explicitly calls Him the supreme spiritual master, *Guru-śreṣṭha*—the best of the Gurus, full of sweetness and strength, who connects us back to Kṛṣṇa. 

But why did Balarāma go alone? Why did Kṛṣṇa stay back? 

The Yadus in Dvārakā knew the secret: Kṛṣṇa is completely subservient to pure love. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura explains their reasoning directly: the Lord's beloved gopīs are famous for their pure, ecstatic love, and His parents are extremely affectionate toward Him—but Kṛṣṇa Himself is controlled by that very love [11]. If Kṛṣṇa went back to Vṛndāvana even for a single second, He would be so conquered by their love that He would never be able to leave. 

Kṛṣṇa Himself revealed His heart to His queen Satyabhāmā: *“Satyabhāmā, I am not going back to Vṛndāvana. It is not because I do not love them. If I go back there, they will be filled with the ultimate happiness. But when the time comes for Me to leave again, they will suffer a pain that is ten times more devastating than the first heartbreak. I cannot repeat my bad nature. I cannot torture them twice. Therefore, to protect them from a double heartbreak, I stay back in Dvārakā and send my brother Balarāma to console them [8].”* 

Balarāma went to Vṛndāvana and spent months holding the Gopīs, weeping with them, and comforting them with the sweet words of Kṛṣṇa’s instructions. This is the emotional climax of His service. Balarāma has the strength to console us in separation.

---

## **The Strength of Service**

This is the path of Balarāma. We must understand that His strength is not physical muscle or material power—it is **strength in service**. 

* **Strength to serve Kṛṣṇa** in every way, expanding His own body into His brother's dhotī, bed, flute, and crown.
* **Strength to do what is necessary**, even when it requires dragging the Yamunā or bringing Kṛṣṇa back to His duty on Kaṁsa’s battlefield.
* **Strength to pull us from illusion**, cutting away our intellectual pride with His mighty plow.
* **Strength to console us in separation**, holding our broken hearts and carrying us back to Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet.

Let us take shelter of this path of strength. Let us stop relying on our material qualifications and surrender completely to the lotus feet of Balabhadra, who alone holds the key.
