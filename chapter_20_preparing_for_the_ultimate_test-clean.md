# Chapter 20: Preparing for the Ultimate Test

What is the duty of a person who is about to die? 

This is the supreme question of an intelligent person (*manīṣiṇām*) like Parīkṣit Mahārāja—it is precisely because he asked this question that Śukadeva Gosvāmī glorifies him as intelligent. An intelligent person is one who is asking how to quit the material body and prepare for their next destination. Of course, we are asking so many questions every day, but the most important question is never asked. Why is this question the most important question, and why is it not being asked? Because death determines our next destination [1]. As the soul continues to change the body, a destination is prepared. 

Throughout the whole world, people are living their lives with no preparation for death at all. Nobody thinks about it, because everyone assumes they are going to live forever. But eternal life is not found in the material world, nor in this temporary material body—eternal life exists only in the spiritual world, which is what everyone, whether they know it or not, is actually searching for. We want to live eternally, but that is simply not possible here.

---

## **The Lump of Flesh**

Look at our struggles in this material world. All our struggles from morning to night—what are they for? They are for this temporary body. One is going to school, one is going to work, one is sleeping, one is going to eat. Whatever we do in this world, it is strictly to maintain this body. 

But let me ask you: suppose someone delivered a lump of dead flesh to your door. Just a lump of flesh. What would you do with it? You would throw it away! You would say, “This is useless, it is stinking!” 

So, what makes the body important? If someone delivers a live body, we care for it, we dress it, we maintain it. But what are we actually maintaining? The body, which is in itself just a temporary lump of flesh that will eventually be thrown away. That is what we are busy doing from morning to night! But the spirit soul—the actual spark of life that makes this lump of flesh important—is completely forgotten. Nobody cares for the spirit soul. That is a massive, universal tragedy.

In big, prestigious universities all over the world, there is not one single department dedicated to studying the spirit soul [2]. In fact, in many modern schools, even to talk about religion or God is strictly forbidden. Gurukula was not established by scientists; it was established by great, self-realized sages to teach the science of the soul. But now our schools are in the hands of the scientists—whom Śrīla Prabhupāda boldly called the modern demons! So it is not a surprise that there is no spiritual knowledge in our classrooms. Even to talk about God, you have to take special, rare permission, while other dry subjects require no permission at all. 

I remember visiting a university in Brazil, where the assigned topic of my lecture was "The Importance of Religion in Our Life." I looked at the students, I looked at the teachers, and I couldn't help but laugh. 

They looked at me and said, “Maharaj, why are you laughing? You have to speak on the subject!” 

I told them: “I am laughing because of the subject, and because of all of you! You are asking the importance of religion in your life! You don't ask the importance of geography in your life. To study to become a first-class barbarian, you have to pay money for it! But to study to become a pure devotee, you must take special permission! Without permission, you are not allowed. If you want to become a first-class fool, you are glorified, honored, and given degrees. That is the tragedy of modern civilization!”

---

## **The Blindness of Material Civilization**

This material civilization is completely blind because nobody is trying to understand who we are. There are many people who theoretically try to understand that we are not the body. But it is extremely rare to find those who understand that we are also not the mind. The mind is still material; both the gross body and the subtle body are material. 

Look very carefully: every single thing we are doing in this material world is detected by our senses, not by the soul. Can you mention one activity we are doing today which is detected and guided by the soul? Our eating? Our sleeping? All our thinking, feeling, and willing are within the gross and subtle bodies. When are we going to live a life which is detected and guided by the spirit soul? Sincere Sādhana is what begins to awaken the soul.

That awakening, however, does not come from scripture study alone. One may certainly consult revealed scriptures like the *Vedānta-sūtra*, *Bhagavad-gītā*, and *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*—but even after reading and hearing such scriptures, unless one is in touch with a realized spiritual master, one cannot actually realize the true nature of the self. That is precisely why the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* explains that Kṛṣṇa, out of His own causeless mercy, prepared the Vedic literatures through Vyāsadeva—for the sake of an intelligent class of men who had become almost completely forgetful of their genuine relationship with Him [3]. It is only when we are fortunate enough to meet a pure devotee of Kṛṣṇa that we are trained to live a life of the soul [4]. That training begins with the simple recognition, *aham brahmāsmi*—I am spirit, not this body—and continues through the constant hearing and chanting of Kṛṣṇa's glories, the actual activity of the soul.

The entire process of bhakti-yoga is nothing but the revival of this lost relationship with Kṛṣṇa. And this revival is only possible in the human form of life, a body obtained only after the long evolutionary cycle of 8.4 million species. This is why the human birth is so precious—and exactly why we cannot afford to let it be wasted.

But this does not mean that everyone must abandon their prescribed duty to sit and do nothing but hear and chant. Even Brahmā, the most intelligent being in the universe, once became angry when the four Kumāras wished to remain celibate and dedicate themselves purely to hearing and glorifying Kṛṣṇa, instead of marrying and continuing the family line as he wished. Was Brahmā wrong to want this? No—as a Prajāpati, a householder, his duty was to procreate and expand creation, and that was exactly what his time and position required of him. What is required of an intelligent person changes according to place, time, and circumstance. It is not a contradiction; it is simply that each of us must sincerely fulfill our own position while keeping our consciousness fixed on Kṛṣṇa.

---

## **The False Promises of Modern Civilization**

The politicians, the philosophers, and the scientists are dangerous, first-class cheaters [5]. They constantly keep us in anxiety. That is their business—they are in anxiety, and they are busily distributing their anxiety to everyone else. 

The philosophers will tell you that you are God, the politicians will promise you free education and facilities if you vote them into power, and the scientists will tell you: “Don't worry! We are going to take you to the moon very soon. We are making arrangements to make you deathless!” 

And look at their Samsung phones. Every year they release Samsung X20, Samsung X22, 24, 26, 30, 50, 100! People run to buy them. But let me ask you: what is the actual difference between Samsung 30 and Samsung 100? It has the same communication, the same internet, the same browser! The only difference is the name, the price, and the artificial anxiety they plant in your head to make you believe that 100 is somehow better than 30! It is the same anxiety changing shape for you. 

Even Lord Brahmā, who lives the longest life in this universe, felt miserable and full of anxiety. During the creation, before Lord Brahmā had even finished his work, a powerful demon suddenly appeared [6]. Lord Brahmā was terrified. He saw it and began to run for his life in sheer fear! Lord Viṣṇu Himself was calling out to him, “Brahmā, come! I have already killed the demon!” But Brahmā was so bewildered by fear that he didn't listen; he kept running! 

If Lord Brahmā himself, who lives for 311 trillion years, is running in fear, what about us? This material world is a place where fear and anxiety are inescapable. But an intelligent person is one who realizes that they must get out of this anxiety-ridden prison and revive their lost relationship with Kṛṣṇa.

---

## **Drinking the Nectar of Kṛṣṇa-kathā**

How do we practically prepare for the moment of death? It is compulsory that we hear *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* attentively, every single day.

When Parīkṣit Mahārāja sat on the bank of the Ganges, having been cursed to die in seven days, thousands of great sages gathered around him. What did they recommend? 

Some of them came and said, “Maharaj, don't worry, we are going to give you *amṛta*—immortality.” But they were talking about physical longevity in this material world! Sages who are in reality and engaged in the activity of Brahman have no material solution to death. The only true answer came through his own spiritual master, Śukadeva Gosvāmī, who gave him the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* itself—the continuous hearing of the activities of Kṛṣṇa [7].

And look at the miracle that took place! Parīkṣit Mahārāja sat on the bank of the Ganges for seven days and seven nights. He did not take a single drop of water, he did not eat, and he did not sleep. 

If an ordinary person is told they are going to die, they will lose their appetite out of sheer fear. But Parīkṣit Mahārāja was not losing his appetite because of fear. He told Śukadeva Gosvāmī: “I am not hungry or thirsty, because I am drinking nectar. I am drinking the *Hari-kathāmṛta*. And because of this nectar, I have completely forgotten all external, material tastes. I have no more taste for anything else [8].”

This is genuine hearing. Hearing should take us to the point that we lose our taste for material enjoyment. The body should not sit in the class; the mind must sit in the class and hear. If the mind sits in the class and hears, then it is hearing. One who truly hears will experience a powerful reaction. Parīkṣit Mahārāja had this reaction: “You have planted Kṛṣṇa in my heart.” And because Kṛṣṇa was planted in his heart, he became completely fearless. He was ready for anything. Hearing had put an end to all his material miseries. This is hearing—it is not just a show, it is a reality.

---

## **The Absolute Consistency of a Pure Devotee**

If you want to know a pure devotee, you must check their consistency. Genuine bhakti is reflected in consistent behavior. 

What was the speaker doing before the lecture? What would he do after the lecture? Check and see if they are consistent. If their behavior is different, that is not a pure devotee. We must not confuse material skills or academic eloquence with pure devotion. A speaker may speak very nice philosophy. But does he practice what he is speaking? He is speaking for the audience, but he doesn't even practice it! 

To read something and repeat it is not a problem. To realize it and live it—that is the real challenge. And that is extremely rare. 

A pure devotee's private life and public life are exactly the same. He doesn't have a double, compromised life. Public life is the same, private life is the same. Consistent. 

Do not just check the outside dress. Anyone can put on a dress. Will you know what is inside my body? Will you know if my body is full of scars? If I cover my body with a shirt and show my teeth to you, does that mean I am a happy person? No. Check if the heart is happy—that is the real test. The outward smile, anyone can do. Bhakti is not a cosmetic, plastic smile. If you want to know bhakti, check if it is consistent.

Let us stop playing games. Let us take our Sādhana seriously, hear with our minds sitting in the class, and let Kṛṣṇa be planted in our hearts so we can perfectly pass our final examination at the moment of death.
